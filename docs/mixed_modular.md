# A Boolean bridge between arithmetic modulo two and modulo three

Running two Gaussian eliminations independently can miss a contradiction even when every local relation is understood in its own characteristic. The bridge uses the **same Boolean assignments** in the two reasoning systems. It does not add residues from different rings.

## An example where the shared variables matter

Give two otherwise disjoint flow networks three shared Boolean port bits x,y,z. One bank extends exactly the port words of odd weight. The other extends exactly those whose weight is 2 modulo 3. In three bits the latter means exactly two ones, an even weight. Each bank separately has solutions; in fact neither pins any single port. There is no common solution. Changing the second demand to 1 allows all three single-one port words and explicit extensions in both banks. The [encoding and the spanning-tree extension](ternary_flow.md) work on arbitrary prism lengths in the stated range. The algorithm sees no port IDs or hint about the split.

After summing the learned star constraints, the mod-3 equation x+y+z=2 is available. On Boolean values it implies x+y+z=0 modulo 2. Sharing this equation conflicts with the first bank. At the *interface alone* no nontrivial unary or binary affine equation distinguishes these cases: the odd relation projects onto all pair words and the weight-two relation projects onto three noncollinear Boolean points at every pair. With four shared ports the same mod-3 relation still means exactly two ones, but all its projections onto three ports have full affine span (weights one or two); it gives a width-four bridge into even parity. The [report](../results/mixed_modular.json) separately tests the actual algorithm on both full CNFs: on families of sizes k=4,7,12, the smaller width is UNKNOWN and the indicated width finds a checked proof. The proof about minimal interface arity does not rule out other proofs using internal network coordinates.

In an additional 445-variable, 2,040-clause example, five ternary banks force five triples to have weight two, five binary banks give a terminal bit equal to the parity of each triple, and a final ternary bank demands that the sum of the terminals be 1. The triples force all terminals to zero, contradicting the final bank. An actual checked DAG uses both 3→2 and 2→3 changes of field, and takes two scans of the two spaces. With a final demand of zero we verified an original satisfying word. These figures describe this implementation, not a universal lower bound on rounds. A refuter returning UNKNOWN on a positive control is not a SAT certificate; the separate word is.

## Finding the rows without knowing the interface

Start from all [local hull rows](modular_hull.md) over each field. Elimination stores linear combinations with provenance. In a consistent space express coordinate x_i as b_i + v_i·z, with z the unrestricted free coordinates **of that field relaxation**. Thus a candidate sum with coefficients c_i is constant iff the corresponding signatures v_i cancel; its right side is Σ c_i b_i. We enumerate normalized left sides through four terms. Equality lookups find pairs; hashing sums of pairs finds triples or quadruples. A GF(2) parity on three or more variables implies no nontrivial affine equation over GF(3) on its own Boolean cube: pair-flips preserve parity, and comparison at complementary local bit patterns forces the coefficients to vanish. We skip only those unproductive transfer shapes. The search charges signature probes and short-row visits separately; optional caps do not bound initial local table extraction or all bit operations. Exhausting a cap yields UNKNOWN unless a contradiction was already proved.

For each candidate row, enumerate the at most 16 Boolean words on its support. Compute the other field's affine equations on the surviving words and insert any independent consequences into that field. Continue until contradiction, an optional cap, or a full pass with no rank increase. There are at most 2n rank increases before contradiction. At maximum support four and fixed input width, scope discovery, local truth tables, finite-field matrix arithmetic, sparse combination enumeration and certificate replay all take polynomial time in the explicit input length (the polynomial need not be small). This is a *partial* method. There is no polynomial bound on a complete general SAT solver from it.

The [checker](../src/pnp_lab/mixed_modular.py) accepts only three types of DAG node: an original-clause Boolean truth table implying a modular equation, a weighted sum in one field, or a cross-field implication verified on the same support's Boolean words. It validates the final 0=1 and never trusts discovery statistics. A falsified cross-field consequence or an unproved circular premise is rejected. For the [order-four family](four_flow.md) even adding arbitrarily many local prime-field consequences and exchanging facts with unrestricted support is insufficient in the stated scope regime: one common Boolean word satisfies *all* those facts. That word does not satisfy the original formula.
