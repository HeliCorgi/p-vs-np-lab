# Recovering an order-four local law without guessing the modulus

[The prime-spectrum test](prime_spectrum.md) identifies fields with additional local affine laws, but characteristic 2 alone need not say which power of 2 matters. We compute an integer invariant of the Boolean model table and extract its modular equations. On signed-renamed stars the result distinguishes **one order-four law**, **two independent order-two laws**, and **only one order-two law**. The first and third examples have identical matrix ranks not only modulo 2 but over the rationals and **every** prime field.

[Integer reducer and trace checker](../src/pnp_lab/local_torsion.py) · [finite experiment](../experiments/local_torsion.py) · [raw certificates and extracted coefficients](../results/local_torsion.json) · [tests](../tests/test_local_torsion.py).

## What is recovered?

On a named scope of d Boolean variables form one integer row (x,1) per **distinct** model x of all input clauses wholly contained in the scope. If L is the integer row lattice, consider the finitely generated abelian group

    H = Z^(d+1) / L.

A homomorphism H→Z/m is exactly an affine coefficient vector modulo m vanishing on every model: its values on the coordinate generators give the coefficients, with the last coefficient the constant offset. H is not a set of Boolean solutions or a witness to their global compatibility. The empty table has no relations imposed on the coefficient vector, vacuously; it is explicitly marked empty, rather than evidence for a special flow order.

If U M V has positive nonzero diagonal s₁|...|s_r and all other entries zero, where U,V are unimodular, then H has free rank d+1−r with finite torsion factors Z/s_i for s_i>1. The free generators already give affine laws characteristic-independently. The finite factors describe additional characters: in Z/m, the images allowed for a generator of order s are exactly the gcd(s,m) elements killed by s. The list of exceptional primes discards the exponents. A field's rank drop counts how many s_i are divisible by its characteristic, also discarding their exponents. In particular Z/4 and Z/2 yield one extra binary equation each; over fields of odd characteristic neither gives a rank drop. Z/2⊕Z/2 gives two extra binary equations, but no character generating an order-four image on the torsion part. Free generators can themselves be mapped to order four modulo four; such characters are not what the term **torsion factor** refers to here.

The column calculation also gives explicit equations. For s_j>1 the original coordinates of column j of V, reduced modulo s_j, yield a coefficient vector v with Mv=0 mod s_j. This follows because U(MV_j)=s_j e_j, and U is unimodular. The recorded coefficients (last entry moved to the right side) can be tested against raw Boolean models without interpreting a hidden alphabet. The basis choices and actual coefficient representatives need not be unique; the invariant factors are.

## Why a four-state addition table stores its group as torsion

More generally let G be a finite abelian group and write three ports a,b,c in one-hot coordinates. Use all local words with c=a+b. A row in the presentation of H says

    A_a + B_b + C_(a+b) + J = 0,

where J denotes the constant coordinate generator. The row at (0,0) eliminates J, leaving the three base generators A_0,B_0,C_0 free. Subtract the base generator from each of its port's other generators. Writing the differences as A′, B′, C′, the remaining equations say A′_a + B′_b + C′_(a+b)=0. Taking a=0 or b=0 eliminates A′_a and B′_b in favor of −C′_a and −C′_b. The residual presentation C′_0=0, C′_(a+b)=C′_a+C′_b is **exactly G**: send C′_a to a in G, and inversely send a in G to C′_a. Thus H≅Z³⊕G, not merely a group whose order is divisible by |G|. Orienting a port oppositely, changing the modular charge, or arbitrarily renaming its states permutes indicator columns. Permuting or complementing Boolean variables is likewise an invertible integer transformation on augmented coordinates: complement replaces a column by the constant column minus that column. These transformations preserve the quotient up to isomorphism.

The four-state width-three encoder also includes one helper per port. On a satisfying one-hot word, the helper equals the **integer sum** of the two indicators in its second half. Subtracting those two columns clears the helper column by unimodular operations, which adds one free generator and no finite factor. The observed 15-bit stars therefore have free rank six and torsion G. For the reduced relation which enforces only the parity of a+b−c while leaving the upper state bit arbitrary, comparing words that differ only in the forgotten bit identifies either state's coordinate generator with the other of the same parity. The remaining presentation is the order-two case, with free rank six but only Z/2 torsion. This explains the all-prime collision between [4] and [2]. The cyclic and Klein controls each have **16** Boolean models; the parity-only star has 32.

## Discovery, replay and limits

The scanner proposes unions of three four-state one-hot blocks from [the existing incidence recognizer](cyclic_four.md): it sees clauses and shared Boolean variables, not states numbered by an external group or group operations. An explicit scope can alternatively be queried. The integer reducer itself works on arbitrary Boolean tables of width at most 15. It reads local clauses using Boolean bit columns, computes exact Smith form by Euclidean row/column swaps, additions of integer multiples of a *different* row/column, and row sign changes. If an isolated pivot fails to divide the remaining block, adding an offending row to the pivot row exposes a nonmultiple; Euclidean division then strictly lowers the pivot magnitude. Completed positive pivots divide subsequent blocks. This is standard Smith normal form, not a new general-purpose polynomial-bit algorithm. For established polynomial algorithms see [Kannan and Bachem, SIAM J. Computing, 1979](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040).

The checker independently branches on raw Boolean coordinates to enumerate the models, reconstructs M, replays only the permitted unimodular operations, and tests the positive divisibility diagonal and every off-diagonal zero. There is no trust in a proposed group label or a floating-point rank computation. Tests also compare **all 256** three-bit tables against a separate gcd-of-all-minors calculation using permutation determinants at every rank. Local model count (default at most 256), number of operations (20,000), intermediate bit length (512), and number of scanned scopes are visible limits. Exceeding a reducer cap gives UNKNOWN and is not recorded as absence of torsion. The scope cap concerns candidate scope traversal, not the time to recognize domains. Local Boolean table size is still exponential in width, capped at 15 here. These bounds and the simple integer eliminator do not assert polynomial behavior for arbitrary global matrix dimension.

The [reported run](../results/local_torsion.json) recovered [4] on all 44 proposed stars of balanced and unbalanced signed-renamed networks (up through 120 Boolean variables in the network). On three separate 15-bit comparison inputs it recovered [4], [2,2], [2], and independently evaluated the extracted modular rows on models found by enumerating all 98,304 raw assignments across the three inputs. Neither a local torsion order nor an extracted local generator settles SAT: aligning equations across shared ports and deriving a checked contradiction or a model are separate tasks. Globally new torsion can arise on composition even when each local row lattice has trivial torsion, as the odd-cycle control in the [prime-spectrum experiment](prime_spectrum.md) illustrates.
