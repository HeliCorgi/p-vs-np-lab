# All tag-prefix branches can be pairwise hard for resolution-justified transport

Consider direct comparison of two residuals by substituting signed source literals or constants for target variables. A nontrue pulled-back target clause must now be justified by **any ordinary resolution proof from the source**, including shared intermediate clauses. Allow weakening. Unlike raw subsumption this permits multiple input clauses and unlimited proof width. We measure **total ordinary resolution lines** across the whole comparison, counting a shared line once. The conclusion applies to this specific method and measure.

For arbitrarily large k there is a width-three CNF of size M=Θ(k²) clauses with k designated selector bits. At every depth 1≤d≤k of the selector-first tree, for ANY two distinct raw residuals, in EITHER direction, EVERY such proof-carrying substitution needs exp(Ω(k)) lines (and maximum width Ω(k)). Hence all 2^d residuals are mutually incomparable using certificates of polynomial resolution length. The length bound per directed comparison is exp(Ω(√M)); in the ordinary O(M log M) literal-index bit encoding it is exp(Ω(√(L/log L))). The property also holds for designated occurrence-copy selectors of the [blind encoding](split_parity.md) with at most one clause per unsigned triple. The polynomial overheads below never enter the exponent.

**What this does not establish.** The inputs are deliberately assembled from independently UNSAT modules. One need not explore the entire frontier, and an algorithm exploiting that decomposition or the underlying parity contradiction may decide much earlier. No bounds on arbitrary orderings, anchored-disjunction labels, extended resolution, Gaussian elimination, other global inference, or SAT itself follow. These are mathematical existence families, not a claim of a deterministic polynomial-time generator of the large graph library. The [port antichain](port_antichain.md) is a complementary comparison; the obstruction here specifically requires ordinary-resolution justifications and is strong in *both* directions at *every* positive tag depth.

## Building enough genuinely different modules

Use the [robust local-goal lemma](robust_ceiling.md): for a simple cubic q-vertex expander G of uniform positive edge expansion h whose cuts of size at most three always isolate a vertex, take the odd-charge Tseitin 3-CNF T(G). It has m=4q clauses and 3q/2 edge variables and is clause-minimal UNSAT. After fixing at most three edge bits consistently with each original clause, every resolution refutation of the resulting CNF has width Ω(q) and length exp(Ω(q)), uniformly over G. Here “consistently” means *no whole original clause is falsified by the partial assignment*. The odd giant component survives pinning; see the proof and Ben-Sasson–Wigderson references in the linked lemma. For minimality, deleting one vertex clause allows prescribing its unique bad local pattern and solving the remaining even-demand parity system on the connected graph with that vertex removed.

We need 2k **pairwise nonisomorphic** graphs of one order q=Θ(k), not just two. As justified in the same note by the [random regular graph eigenvalue theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04482) and the [short-cycle distribution](https://users.monash.edu/~nwormald/papers/girth.pdf), a fixed positive limiting fraction (or a constant lower bound for all large even orders) of uniformly labelled simple cubic graphs has expansion at least h=1/20 and fixed girth at least 62. Such a graph has the required cuts: a side with at most q/2 vertices and at most three boundary edges has ≤60 vertices, induces a forest, and must be a singleton by the cubic degree sum. The expansion holds with probability 1−o(1); no independence of expansion and girth events is required.

Here is an elementary count to turn positive fraction into many types. Take q divisible by four. Start with any ordered Hamilton cycle on q labelled vertices. Pair the first consecutive quarter with the third and the second quarter with the fourth via arbitrary bijections. The cycle together with this perfect matching is simple and cubic for q≥8. There are q!·((q/4)!)² choices of ordered vertex list and matchings. A resulting labelled cubic graph admits at most q·3·2^(q−1) ordered Hamilton cycles (choose the start, the first neighbor, and then at most two choices per step); once the ordered cycle is given the matching is determined. Thus the number of **all** simple labelled cubic graphs, divided by q!, is at least ((q/4)!)²/(3q·2^(q−1)). This lower bound grows faster than any polynomial in q. A positive fraction of the labelled population is good and each isomorphism type has at most q! labellings. There are consequently more than 2q good nonisomorphic types at all sufficiently large such orders. We can take k=q and select types G_i^0,G_i^1 for 1≤i≤k. We do not require that the good graphs themselves contain Hamilton cycles; those were used only to lower-bound the whole population.

## A disjoint source cannot cheaply generate a missing module

First work before introducing selector CNFs. Let S be a conjunction of k variable-disjoint T(G)'s from distinct chosen types. Let T be one other missing type. Suppose a map on the variables of T provides proofs from S of its nontrue pulled-back clauses.

1. Let D be a nontrue such clause (width ≤3) containing no clause of S. Pin its variables to falsify it. Separate component by component: each source received at most three pins that do not falsify one of its clauses, so its restricted formula still has an exponentially hard resolution refutation by the local-goal lemma. A derivation of D (or any subclause) from S restricts to a refutation of their disjoint union, of no greater length or width. Strip weakenings by retaining subclauses as in the [substitution lemma](width_transfer.md). Without weakening a derivation from variable-disjoint components never mixes nonempty clauses from distinct components: a resolution pivot must occur on both sides. Any first empty clause has therefore already refuted one component. This gives the same exponential length and linear width lower bounds for deriving D. Fresh unused variables in D do not change the argument.
2. If every nontrue goal instead contains a source clause, choose one such input clause per goal. The chosen subset of S must be UNSAT, since its model would transport to a model of T. In a variable-disjoint union of minimally UNSAT components that subset must contain **every** clause of at least one component. There are m target clauses and m clauses in each component. Thus all m goals must be nontrue, use every clause of one source component once, and include no source clause from another component. All clauses are ternary. Each target image must have length three and equal its chosen source clause exactly; no constant, cancellation, or repeated literal can shorten it. All 3q/2 source component variables are hit and the target has just that many active variables, so the substitution on them is a signed bijection. Equality of unsigned supports gives a graph isomorphism: every distinct triple of incident variables represents one vertex and each edge variable joins the two triples in which it occurs. This contradicts the missing type.

Combining 1 and 2, every map needs at least one genuinely hard goal. No counting of possible maps, or assumption that the search algorithm is inefficient, is involved.

## Put all comparisons in one input and lift the bound through encoding

For module i, a fresh selector s_i chooses T(G_i^0) or T(G_i^1) on otherwise variable-disjoint scopes. Guard each clause of side b by the literal l_s false when s_i=b. Replace the guarded ternary l_s∨a∨b'∨c by (l_s∨a∨y)∧(¬y∨b'∨c), using a private y per clause. This gives Θ(kq) proper clauses and variables. Let distinct depth-d words x,z fix the same first d selectors and let i be a position where they differ.

On the **source** side extend x arbitrarily to a full selector word, choose the k corresponding Tseitin components to form S, and lift symbolically from S to that raw prefix. Set all selectors as chosen, use the component bits for active graph edges, set unused edge bits arbitrarily, set each active helper y=¬a, and set each inactive helper false. Every source-prefix clause now becomes true or an actual S axiom. Any shared proof DAG from that prefix can be pulled back to S without increasing its length or width, or discarded if a line becomes true.

On the **target** side s_i is already fixed. From its two active half clauses for each original triple, resolve once to obtain the clause of T(G_i^{z_i}); ignore every other module and unresolved selector. This costs O(q) ordinary lines. Transport these steps through the candidate map using its supplied proofs, and then through the source lift. This provides a signed/constant map from S to a **missing** type together with proofs of its nontrue goals at cost at most the offered length plus O(q), and width at most the maximum of the offered width and 3. The preceding lower bound applies. Swapping x and z gives exactly the same argument, at every d. Distinct partial words need not be assigned consistent completions on the two sides; the chosen *fixed mismatching position* is precisely why the argument works before the full tag cut.

Finally apply the previous distinct-support reduction E to the whole input and fix one occurrence copy of each of the first d selectors. The source lift also sets the six gadget bits and final filler bits true, and maps every occurrence copy to its chosen original literal/constant, so all source encoding clauses pull back to true or an S axiom. On the target side derive the forcing units using the constant-size checked gadget proofs, remove false pads, and propagate the chosen selector copy around its equality cycle. Occurrences needed for the active module clauses can be rewritten to their representative via cycle implications derived by ordinary resolution: a path of length r costs O(r) lines of width two; resolving into a width-three clause does not increase its width. Reconstruct the target triples as above. The overhead is polynomial in kq and width at most 6; the constant-width gadget proofs and path argument are detailed in [the two-way result](robust_ceiling.md). This applies to ordinary DAG proofs, so intermediate clauses shared between multiple transported goals are counted only once.

[Finite tests](../tests/test_layered_barrier.py) construct a genuine two-tag, four-graph control, not a large expander experiment. For four simple cubic graphs on 10 vertices they verify every possible small-side subset of sizes 2–5 has at least four boundary edges and distinguish the types by 4-, 5-, and 6-cycle counts. The resulting 222-variable, 320-clause raw input is checked at every prefix of all four completions: all clauses pull back to true or a selected input axiom, both before and after the distinct-support encoding. These checks validate the constructions and quantifiers in small cases, not an exponential proof-size estimate on these graphs.
