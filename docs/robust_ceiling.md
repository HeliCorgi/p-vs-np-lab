# A two-way barrier even for ordinary resolution proofs of transported clauses

**Claim.** For all sufficiently large even q there exist two sibling residuals of a single width-three CNF of size O(q) clauses, such that in **either direction**, for **every** substitution of signed source variables or constants for target variables, ordinary resolution proofs justifying the substituted target clauses require total size exp(Ω(q)). Shared proof lines do not evade the bound. The same holds in sibling residuals of the [distinct-unsigned-triples encoding](split_parity.md). Size here counts proof lines; q counts graph vertices. In a binary variable-index encoding of input length L=O(q log q) this gives exp(Ω(L/log L)), not an assertion of exp(Ω(L)). The result concerns **this proof system**. Both siblings are UNSAT, so every substitution is semantically a witness map vacuously. Detecting their global parity contradictions is easy outside ordinary resolution.

We use the standard Tseitin width and resolution size–width theorems of [Ben-Sasson and Wigderson, Theorems 4.4 and 3.5](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/emo/SatSem05/Papers/BensassonWidgerson01.pdf). The arguments below are a specialization and composition of known machinery, not a claim of a new general resolution lower bound or of literature priority. Restriction and substitution of a resolution proof may discard tautologies and retain subclauses; [the projection lemma and executable checker](width_transfer.md) give the details. Weakening does not invalidate the lower bound (remove it by the same subclause argument). A proof using auxiliary *extension axioms*, Gaussian elimination steps, or another stronger logic is not covered.

## 1. Many clauses are hard to derive for the same source

Let G be a connected simple cubic graph on q vertices with edge expansion at least h>0: every nonempty S of at most q/2 vertices has at least h|S| edges leaving S. Assume every edge cut of size at most three isolates a vertex on one side. Put a bit on each edge and a parity equation on the three bits at each vertex, using odd total charge. Write the equations as four ternary clauses per vertex, one for each forbidden local assignment. Their conjunction T(G) is UNSAT: summing all equations counts each edge twice.

**Local rigidity lemma.** If D is a non-tautological clause of width at most three which contains no clause of T(G), proving D from T(G) by ordinary resolution needs exp(Ω(q)) lines and width Ω(q), with constants uniform for fixed h. This includes the empty clause. Set the at most three distinct edge bits to falsify D. Delete those edges and adjust each endpoint's charge by its pinned bit. The restricted parity CNF, after dropping true clauses and repeated literals/clauses, is the Tseitin CNF on the remaining graph, up to true isolated equations.

* If the remaining graph is disconnected, it consists of one isolated vertex and a connected remainder for q sufficiently large: a smallest component has at most q/2 vertices, its cut has size at most three, hence it is a singleton. Its three incident edges exhaust the deletion budget. A further component would also define a cut of size at most three, forcing another singleton or a co-singleton; three edges cannot isolate two vertices in a simple cubic graph. If the isolated parity were violated, then D would be exactly the forbidden star clause for those three pins, contrary to hypothesis.
* The total charge is still odd, since each deleted edge changes two charges. The remaining connected graph on q or q−1 vertices therefore has odd total charge. For a set S of at most half its vertices the new boundary is at least h|S|−3. If |S|≥6/h this is at least h|S|/2. For smaller nonempty S, connectivity supplies at least one edge. Thus the large component still has positive uniform expansion, or equivalently has Ω(q) boundary on all balanced cuts.

Theorem 4.4 gives Ω(q) required width there; initial width is at most three and there are O(q) edge variables, so Theorem 3.5 yields exp(Ω(q)) size. Any short resolution proof of D would restrict under the pins to a short refutation. This proves the lemma simultaneously for **all** such D, without paying a union bound or assuming hardness for a random choice of D.

## 2. Why neither direction works even when the map is chosen optimally

Take two such graphs G,H on the same q vertices, nonisomorphic as abstract graphs. The old [direct certificate rigidity argument](transport_obstruction.md) also applies here. T(G) is clause-minimally UNSAT: deleting any one star clause permits that forbidden local pattern. The remaining charge system can be satisfied because G−v is connected. Indeed a cut vertex of a cubic G would split its neighbors among at least two components, one joined by at most one edge, contradicting 3-edge connectivity. Fix the three incident edges to violate just the removed clause; the parity demand on G−v is even, so solve along a spanning tree.

Suppose every target clause under a map were true, tautological or contained a source clause. Select one justifying source clause for each nontrivial pulled target clause. This subset of source clauses implies satisfiability of the target under substitution whenever the subset is satisfiable, so it must itself be UNSAT. Minimality forces use of all 4q source clauses. Since the target also has exactly 4q clauses of width three, every target clause must cover one different source clause, with no lost literal or extra literal. Every source variable occurs; with equally many active variables on each side, the map must be a signed bijection. The four clauses on each triple identify a vertex and the shared variables between triples identify edges. This would give a graph isomorphism, a contradiction. Thus **some** pulled target clause falls under the local rigidity lemma, no matter how the map is chosen. Both graphs have the same properties, so the result holds simultaneously in both directions. The hard goal can depend on the proposed map.

Existence (not an efficient explicit uniform construction): in a uniform random simple cubic graph, with probability tending to one the nontrivial eigenvalues are bounded by 2√2+o(1) by Friedman's theorem ([Bordenave's proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04482)). The Rayleigh quotient of the centered indicator of S then gives |∂S|≥(3−λ₂)|S|(1−|S|/q)≥|S|/20 for large q on this event. Set h=1/20. Fix g=62. Restrict to graphs with no cycles of lengths 3,...,g−1. This has positive limiting probability; additionally requiring **zero** cycles of length g, or **exactly one**, each still has positive limiting probability by the fixed-length independent Poisson cycle laws ([McKay, Wormald, Wysocka](https://users.monash.edu/~nwormald/papers/girth.pdf)). Both classes therefore contain expanders at the same sufficiently large even orders; their differing cycle counts ensure nonisomorphism. For any cut ≤3, its smaller side has size ≤3/h=60, hence induces a forest. In a cubic graph a nonempty forest on s vertices with c components has boundary s+2c, so that side must be a singleton. This checks the hypotheses rather than assuming a particular small named graph is an asymptotic expander family.

## 3. Put both obstructions under one real branch bit

Gate the two cores on opposite values of a single bit s; use disjoint private helpers. Each gated clause (g∨l₁∨l₂∨l₃), where g is s or its negation, becomes (g∨l₁∨w) and (¬w∨l₂∨l₃). The input remains a 3-CNF. At either child, a signed substitution retracts the source child to its active naked graph: assign w=¬l₁ on each active gate, producing a tautology and its original clause; set w=false on inactive gates. Inactive graph variables can be arbitrary constants. Conversely, in the target child each active naked clause is the one-step resolvent of its two halves.

Given a small collection/DAG of proofs for all target-child clauses after a map, append the mapped one-step derivations of its active naked clauses and project the whole derivation under the source retraction. The composition on the target graph edge variables is still a signed-literal/constant map to source edge variables. Mapping a resolution pivot to a constant or merging variables may delete a tautology or yield only a stronger subclause; weakening back to the requested non-tautological goal costs at most one line. Total size increases by at most polynomial additive overhead (and at most a constant-factor simulation of the original lines); width cannot increase beyond the old maximum and the gate derivations' constant width. Section 2 therefore forbids small proofs in either direction. In particular, no discovery oracle for candidate signed maps, even one with unlimited running time, can produce polynomial-size ordinary-resolution justifications here.

For the old distinct-support image, encode the *whole* gated formula by E, then choose one occurrence copy s* as the branch bit. The source retraction also sets the gadget pins to a satisfying pin word, makes other gate copies agree with s*, identifies every occurrence copy with its variable, and satisfies the free tail. In the target residual first derive the pinned units by a constant-size resolution proof over the six gadget variables. Lift the padded cycle implications to binary implications, propagate the gate value from s* around its cycle, and use resolutions along each copy cycle to replace an occurrence by a fixed representative in each source clause. With the representatives for graph and private gate variables this derives each active gate half and then each active naked graph clause, in polynomial total size and constant width. Width ≤6 suffices: the gadget has six variables; cycle path clauses are binary and a variable replacement resolves a binary implication with a clause of width ≤3. All operations act on existing variables only. See [explicit gate/copy proof constructions](width_transfer.md). Compose as above. **The proof concerns one pair of children and the cost of cross-certifying them**, not an exponential number of search nodes in that input. An [amplification](port_antichain.md) demonstrates a larger antichain at a single cut without claiming that a solver must enumerate it.

The implemented RUP/limited-lookahead proof format is covered because a logged contradiction under the at most three negated goal literals and d branch decisions has an ordinary resolution translation: derive implications for the forced units by resolving their reason clauses with preceding units under the fixed decisions, derive the conflict, discharge the decision literals, and resolve the two child conclusions on the split variable. Width at most 3+3+d suffices and translation size is polynomial in the trace length. A full proof of this accounting is in [width transfer](width_transfer.md). Producing more of the same short proofs, caching them, or comparing the siblings in the opposite direction cannot fix this pair. Reading and summing their parity constraints can.

## Concrete checks

[Finite controls](../results/rigid_ceiling.json) use the **Petersen graph and the pentagonal prism**, not alleged asymptotic witnesses. They check the effect of all sets of at most three pinned edges, raw local forbidden clauses, minimality/critical witnesses and the gated presentation. These finite checks illustrate why the isolated-star exception is necessary. The exponential claim follows from the argument and cited theorems, not from timing the small programs.
