# When no local conservation law exists, over any characteristic

The modular-hull solver can add valid rows even on formulas with arbitrarily renamed bits. This note isolates a different obstacle: sometimes **no** such row exists on any of the permitted local subformulas. Enlarging the list of primes, trying all scopes instead of selected scopes, and performing unlimited row operations on the collected rows do not fix that absence. The negative result is about raw clause fragments, not about the whole formula's semantic consequences, derived clauses, nonlinear changes of coordinates, or every UNSAT proof.

* For **each fixed** k, SAT on proper signed 3-CNFs of incidence girth greater than 2k is NP-complete. Every fragment using at most k clauses, or induced by at most k variables, has full affine hull over **every** field, simultaneously. It cannot even entail a nonzero multilinear polynomial equation of degree at most two.
* There exist SAT and UNSAT signings of the **same** simple triple supports on arbitrarily large n for which the same statements hold for **every fragment of at most floor(n/1000) clauses or variables**. This stronger size guarantee is existential, not a claimed NP-hardness reduction with linear locality. The two formulas do not have identical raw signs or local solution sets: their degree-at-most-two vanishing equations are identical, namely trivial.
* At fixed locality one can even condition on a single bit value: no other bit participates in a nontrivial affine equation on that conditioned fragment. When counting a variable scope after conditioning include the pivot in the scope if necessary. These statements rule out nontrivial equations other than Boolean identities (and the pin when explicitly conditioned); they do not rule out deciding these instances by some other reasoning.

## Matching frees a coordinate, or a pair of coordinates

A proper signed ternary clause has three distinct underlying variables. Its incidence graph is bipartite with a vertex for each variable and one for each clause. For a set U of clauses write V(U) for all their variables. Suppose a fragment J satisfies

    |V(U)| ≥ |U| + 2 for each nonempty U contained in J.                  (1)

Select two coordinates i,j (even if they are outside some clauses). Delete i and j from the variable side of the incidence graph. Every U still has at least |U| neighbors, so by [Hall's marriage theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-10.37.26) a matching gives each clause of J its own **distinct** remaining variable. Assign that variable to make its literal true. This single partial word satisfies all clauses regardless of the unchosen bits; in particular **all four** values of (i,j) extend, with the *same* other coordinates. With +1 in place of +2 the same argument frees any chosen one bit. The [implementation](../src/pnp_lab/modular_barrier.py) finds the matching and returns a base word; the certificate can be checked by evaluating the raw clauses on the two or four words.

An affine equation valid on a set containing a one-bit flip in every coordinate has zero coefficient on every coordinate, by subtraction. Nonemptiness then kills its constant term. This holds over any field, and even for affine equations over any commutative ring with unit in the standard Boolean coordinates. For a multilinear polynomial P of degree at most two vanishing on all models, fix i,j. The alternating sum of P on the four words just constructed is precisely the coefficient of x_i x_j. All mixed coefficients vanish; then apply the affine argument. Pair-free squares for all choices imply, after pinning i to either value, a flip in every other coordinate j. When the ambient dimension is 0 or 1 the affine case suffices. Differences of equations are taken as polynomials on Boolean points: we reduce x_i²=x_i and disregard those universal Boolean identities. Degree three is meaningfully different: the product of the three falsity indicators of any clause already vanishes on its models.

If J's incidence graph is a forest, the same holds of every subfragment. An incidence forest of t nonempty ternary clauses, v incident variables and c connected components has 3t edges and v+t−c edges, so v=2t+c≥t+2 for t≥1. For affine rows there is a simple extra witness: root each component at a *variable*. Every clause has two child variables. Set both to satisfy that clause. Each nonroot variable is chosen only once, so the resulting word satisfies at least two literals per clause. Its whole radius-one Hamming ball is satisfying. This forest assertion need not hold for arbitrary binary or one-hot constraints: the width-three/distinct-variable hypothesis is essential.

## A fixed-scope NP-complete subclass

For fixed k, take g=2k. No selection of ≤k clause nodes, or of ≤k variable nodes with clauses wholly contained in the selection, can contain an incidence cycle of length >2k. Thus a graph of girth >2k has the required forest property on *every* such selection, not just connected neighborhoods. Hardness of general CSPs on large-girth structures is prior work; in particular see [Kun, *Constraints, MMSNP and expander relational structures*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1701). For exact signed 3-CNF and the clause-variable incidence convention used here, the following elementary reduction specifies what is preserved. For linear CNF (no two clauses share more than one variable) compare [Porschen, Speckenmeyer and Zhao](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166218X08001509) and [Scheder's existence proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.2336). We do not claim a new high-girth CSP hardness theorem.

First choose a constant-size (depending on k) proper high-girth UNSAT CNF F*. To see existence, on v sufficiently large variables sample each distinct triple independently with probability 96/v². The expected number of triples is asymptotic to 16v and the variance is O(v), so at least 12v appear with probability tending to one. An incidence cycle of length 2j has j distinct cyclic variables, j distinct triples, and at most j other positions to specify the third variables. There are at most v^(2j) ordered candidates; legal candidates each have probability (96/v²)^j. Thus for fixed k the expected number of cycles with 2≤j≤k is O_k(1). With probability tending to one there are fewer than sqrt(v) such cycles; remove one triple per original short cycle, which cannot introduce any new cycle. At least 6v triples remain. Give them independent uniformly random signs. The expected number of models is at most 2^v(7/8)^(6v)<1, so some signing is UNSAT. Fix an inclusion-minimal UNSAT subformula and remove one of its clauses C. The remaining satisfiable gadget P forces *each signed literal of C false* (otherwise the original formula would have a model). Choose one such literal f as the pin. P retains the high girth. This constant could also be obtained by enumerating finite candidates once for the fixed k; the proof gives neither a practical gadget size nor a polynomial method in an input k.

Start with ordinary NP-complete proper 3-SAT (one may remove tautologies and pad short 1/2-clauses by sign variants on fresh distinct variables). Replace each literal occurrence with a private copy of its variable, retaining its sign. For each source variable with r≥2 occurrences, enforce equality of its copies by a directed cycle of implications visiting all r copies, using **k** implication edges and k−1 fresh intermediate bits between each consecutive pair. No cycle is needed for a single occurrence. Make each implication u→z a proper ternary clause (¬u ∨ z ∨ f) using its **own** disjoint copy of P. Apart from that one incidence edge to f's variable, no vertex of this pin gadget connects to the main formula. Every gadget has a model and forces the padding literal false. Source models therefore lift by equal copies and gadget models; any output model restricts to equal values around each implication cycle and yields a source model (unused variables arbitrary).

No short cycle crosses a gadget: its attachment edge is a bridge. In the remaining main incidence graph the original clauses are disjoint stars by occurrence copying. Any cycle must traverse an entire stretched equality segment between occurrence vertices: its internal variables and implication clause nodes have degree two after the attachments are removed. This segment already has 2k incidence edges and its endpoints are distinct, so the full cycle is longer. The number of output clauses/variables is linear in the occurrence count for fixed k. Membership in NP is immediate (proper width and the girth bound can also be checked in polynomial time). This proves the claimed fixed-k NP-completeness. The output has bounded maximum incidence degree depending only on k and the chosen constant pin: no padding bit is shared between gadgets. This is a restriction on problems easy for a specific prepass, not an unconditional time lower bound on SAT.

## Arbitrary linear-sized selections: a signing-independent barrier

Use the same random support p=96/n² before assigning any signs, and set δ=1/1000. With probability tending to one, every nonempty set of t≤δn triples has at least t+2 variables. For t=1 this is automatic. For each t≥2, failure is witnessed by t+1 vertices containing t selected triples. The union bound is

    binom(n,t+1) binom(binom(t+1,3),t) p^t
       ≤ en [ (96 e²/6)(t+1)²/(nt) ]^t ≤ en (267 t/n)^t.

Here (t+1)/t≤3/2. The t=2 term is O(1/n); summing from 3 to sqrt(n) gives O(n (267/sqrt(n))³)=o(1); the remaining tail is at most en times a geometric tail with base 0.267. The same sample has at least 12n triples with probability tending to one. Optionally remove one triple from every incidence cycle up through j=floor(log n/(3 log 96)): the expected total number of such cycles is O(n^(1/3)), so o(n) deletions suffice on a suitable sample. Deletion preserves (1), and leaves ≥6n triples. High girth is not needed for the linear-scale conclusion.

Now keep the same support for both choices of signs. All-positive is satisfiable; the first-moment signing argument above gives an UNSAT signing. Both satisfy the matching argument on all fragments up to δn clauses. A variable scope of s≤δn vertices with s≥1 cannot contain s clauses: a selection of s such triples would use at most s rather than at least s+2 variables. Thus its whole induced formula also satisfies (1). Even an oracle returning *all* valid affine or multilinear quadratic equations of each such raw subformula supplies only trivial equations on either twin, over any characteristic. The SAT/UNSAT difference lies outside that information; this does not show the raw clause lists are indistinguishable to an arbitrary algorithm. A local representation involving cubic products, new indicator coordinates, or consequences already learned from a larger proof is outside this argument.

## Finite, separately certified checks

[Experiment](../experiments/modular_barrier.py) · [JSON with the whole finite refutation](../results/modular_barrier.json) · [tests](../tests/test_modular_barrier.py).

On the 31 nonzero words in F₂⁵, use one triple per projective line {a,b,a XOR b}. No two such triples share two variables. Shuffle and sign once deterministically (seed 0) and take the first 152 clauses. This construction uses GF(2) **only to select triple supports**; each triple contributes a single ordinary OR clause, not a parity equation. The independent raw decision-tree verifier checks the 957-node UNSAT certificate against the original 152 clauses. The stored tree is in preorder: negative tokens split on that variable, first false then true; nonnegative tokens are zero-based raw clause indices falsified on that path. The preceding 151 clauses have a checked model, hence give an explicit reusable false-pin gadget at incidence girth six. The all-positive signing of the same 152 supports has the all-true model.

For a finite all-fragments bound, t linear triples consume 3t distinct pairs, so using ≤t variables is impossible for t≤6. Using ≤t+1 variables is impossible for t≤5 as well: on v=t+1 vertices the clause degree at a vertex is ≤floor((v−1)/2), giving at most floor(v floor((v−1)/2)/3) triples. Thus affine blindness through **six clauses**, and quadratic blindness through **five clauses**, are analytical statements on this finite twin rather than averages of sampled subsets. The measured raw checks comprised 858 one-axis pairs and 5,754 four-word squares; an example stretching reduction produced 258 variables and 1,218 proper clauses, preserved the 4-bit source decisions on lifted words, and its incidence girth was checked. No linear-locality asymptotic instance was enumerated to establish the probability bound.
