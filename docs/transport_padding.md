# A joint obstruction to three pruning rules, and a way out by resolution

The [odd-parity switch family](transport_obstruction.md) rules out all direct clause-subsumption substitutions between distinct tag prefixes. But its affine labels already forget the choice of subdivision lengths. We change just the presentation of its clauses. On the new family, at **every tag-prefix depth d**, the specified solver with affine (or blocked-plus-affine) labels, anchored-OR comparisons, direct substitutions and the *one-pass root split-parity* rule keeps **all 2^d branches** unless a user budget stops it. This is not a lower bound against arbitrary orderings, inference rules, or SAT algorithms. Indeed, polynomially generated private resolution steps followed by the *same* parity rule give a checked refutation at the root.

## The padding and its invariants

For every unguarded original clause C=A∨b (b its last literal, A the preceding one or two literals), put A∨w and ¬w∨b in its place with its own fresh w. Leave every selector-guarded clause unchanged. Call the set of all these split clauses P. All clauses still have width at most three. Quantifying w out gives precisely C: when A is false w must be true and hence b true; when A is true w can be false. There are 28k+58 helpers and the padded instance has **45k+86 variables, 68k+116 clauses**. P is present in every tag residual.

Every residual remains clause-minimal UNSAT. For deleting an unsplit clause, extend its old critical assignment satisfying all other clauses by choosing the helpers separately. For either half of a split C, use the old critical assignment for C; both A and b are false, and w can be chosen to violate only the half to be deleted. Counts of clauses, literal occurrences, and active variables remain equal *within each depth*, because the same unguarded clauses were split in every residual.

In a partial or completed raw formula the helpers are exactly the variables occurring **twice** (counting literal occurrences, not distinct supports). Each port occurs in at least two vertex clauses and at least two wire clauses; every active tag occurs twelve times. Resolve the two clauses on each helper; this recovers the previous raw restriction. Resolution commutes with signed variable permutations, so isomorphic padded residuals would yield isomorphic original residuals. They cannot, by the oriented word reconstruction. The [minimality lemma](transport_obstruction.md) then excludes every direct signed-literal/constant subsumption transport in either direction. A decision-implied map using the fact that the source is UNSAT is not such a certificate.

## Why the implemented root parity rule does not fire

At the root every member of P has a unique support: the two clauses involving a given helper have nonempty, disjoint subsets of the old clause as their remaining literals. Thus none yields an unconditional parity group. Each surviving guarded wire support has just two of the eight local patterns, short of the four required for a complete orbit. A root split on:

* A **tag** produces two zero-charge sets of wire equalities, one matching per side. Every common consequence is homogeneous.
* A **helper** has only one premise clause on each side. Removing its literal exposes either one binary clause (no parity row) or one unit. If both sides give a unit, they concern distinct port variables, so their augmented spans have zero intersection.
* A **port** produces at most helper units on one side and helper units on the other, with disjoint helper IDs. Indeed a port occurs in only one half of any particular split; other restricted padded clauses have a unique helper support and cannot form a complete binary parity group. Each restricted guard clause is a binary clause on (tag, other port), unique on that side because a changed wire gets only one guard orientation and only one forbidden equality pattern survives the pivot. These do not supply binary XOR rows either.

Thus the only common rows come from the tag slices, and all are homogeneous. They cannot sum to 0=1. This argument concerns the actual restricted premises and one pass of [split_parity.py](../src/pnp_lab/split_parity.py); it does not cover unrestricted resolution followed by parity extraction.

## Why raw affine, blocked and anchor comparisons all fail at those layers

Fix two different residuals H_a and H_b at depth d. They share P, the gated wires on the tags not yet assigned, and the three chosen wire equalities at each assigned position where a and b agree. The other clauses are the differing chosen wires. For their disjunction, canonical distribution is the conjunction of these shared clauses plus pairwise products (clause unions) of the unshared clauses. Tautologies and supersets may be removed. Two properties control all subsequent operations:

1. **Each shared clause C is indispensable even after forming the OR.** It cannot be properly subsumed before the OR: both inputs are minimal UNSAT. A smaller product subsuming C would include an input clause properly contained in C, also impossible. Every other clause of the canonical OR contains some clause of H_a other than C: products using C contain it and are removed, except for C itself. A critical assignment for C in H_a satisfies every other canonical clause. Since H_a∨H_b is UNSAT, C belongs to every UNSAT subset of the canonical OR. Deleting blocked clauses preserves unsatisfiability, so cannot delete C during any round. The same indispensability applies to individual raw residuals by their minimality.
2. **The helper-containing clauses P stay outside the XOR subsystem.** Products involving a shared clause contribute no new minimal clause. All surviving extra products thus involve only ports of differing wires, never a helper. Each support in P remains unique, of width at least two, and all ports are mentioned in P through their vertex clauses. Presence of P stops the label from returning YES for an empty nonlinear core. Any extracted row is justified only by non-P clauses. These are consistent, since assigning zero to every port satisfies all wiring for either tag choice. Thus the extracted equations cannot return NO either, irrespective of blocking. All port coordinates remain named in the nonlinear core; the affine projection cannot forget them.

Now consider a specific wire e in the matching chosen by a at assigned position i. If b agrees at i, both binary clauses for e are shared and indispensable. The affine subsystem includes their equality, which remains a consequence of its projected equations because its two ports were not forgotten. If b disagrees at i, choose e among the three changed wires. Its endpoints belong to *different* pairs in b's matching. There is a port assignment satisfying **all b-wiring**, given any completion of its free tags, that assigns different values to those endpoints: each wire just pairs two ports, so set the two wire values differently. This assignment need not satisfy P (the whole formula is UNSAT); it only witnesses that the extracted affine subsystem does not imply equality on e. It satisfies every shared non-P clause and every unshared product because the latter contains a b-wire clause. Blocking can only delete constraints. Hence this assignment satisfies all extracted rows and their projections. The distinction is one of *row-space consequences*, not merely clause ordering.

Consequently for a fixed anchor a the projected rowspace of H_a∨H_b identifies exactly the positions where b differs from a. There are 2^d−1 distinct opaque partner keys for **every** anchor; no anchor collision/terminal marker occurs. The same wire-consequence argument for two individual keys (one fixed matching versus the other) distinguishes all 2^d. It also excludes exact normalized syntax collisions, since syntactic equality would produce equal downstream affine keys. For unbounded search or a cap large enough to reach a layer, induction on d now gives width exactly 2^d up to and including the last tag split. This proof is specific to the documented rules. In particular it excludes a free SAT-status oracle, an SCC/resolution prepass, or arbitrary sound reasoning about P before extracting affine consequences.

## A polynomial escape in the same experiment

Every helper occurs once with each sign. Its two clauses resolve to its original width-two or width-three clause. The [private resolution layer](../src/pnp_lab/private_resolution.py) finds these premises without knowing the tags or helper IDs, adds their original resolvents, and then searches for the common parity refutation. The source clauses are retained, so no step assumes that deleting a premise is safe. After restoration the original complete parity orbits and the guarded conservation equations are available, refuting the instance. The extra split clauses introduce no other complete orbit; in particular their private-support clauses, if seen during a port cut, can introduce at most helper units exclusive to that slice and cannot invalidate the conservation argument. The certificate replayer checks the resolution steps and then checks the final parity proof against their explicitly augmented CNF. This does not rely on graph reconstruction or the experimental name ordering.
