# Recover a modulus-three conservation law hidden in Boolean width-three clauses

The [generator](../src/pnp_lab/ternary_flow.py) supplies fixtures and positive witnesses, **not** a decoder to the [affine-hull learner](modular_hull.md). The learner receives a dimension and signed Boolean clauses only. Arbitrary common variable permutation, independent variable complementation, and clause/literal reordering are allowed. The flow construction is classical: see Alekhnovich and Razborov, [*Lower Bounds for Polynomial Calculus: Nonbinomial Case*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/tm403), for modular Tseitin tautologies and characteristic-specific polynomial-calculus lower bounds. We do not ascribe a general proof-complexity lower bound or novelty to our elementary affine argument.

## Encoding and exact projection

On an oriented edge e put a flow t_e in F3. Encode the three states with indicators I[e,0], I[e,1], I[e,2], one positive ternary and three negative binary clauses enforcing exactly one. Demand at vertex v is the sum of outgoing minus incoming flows. At a cubic vertex forbid each of the 18 of 27 incident triples which violate its specified charge by one all-negative ternary clause selecting those three states. Thus the raw interface is entirely Boolean: 3|E| bits, 4|E|+18|V| clauses. There is exactly one Boolean word per feasible trit flow. The F2 variant instead uses one bit per edge, four clauses per cubic vertex. Neither solver is given the domains. The code accepts any explicit connected cubic graph for these guarantees (and in fact supports ports and degrees up to three); no expansion assumption is required for refuting an imbalance.

Each edge occurs in two divergence equations with opposite signs, so total charge must vanish. Conversely a connected graph realizes any demand with zero total: assign nontree edges zero, solve the parent edge at each leaf, and transfer its remaining demand to the parent. The final root equation is satisfied because the sum is zero. For a positive control we plant a flow using nonzero states and compute its (not identically zero) demands, while its negative twin changes a single charge by one. The experiment verifies the scrambled positive word against the raw clauses instead of interpreting UNKNOWN as SAT.

The three pairwise incompatibilities and ternary clause allow the algorithm to find each unsigned domain candidate even after complementation. The closure of a vertex nogood under its three discovered domains gives the nine-bit star; all its raw clauses are available. The learner obtains a basis of *all* linear forms constant on its local Boolean models by enumeration. In F3 their span includes the divergence equation, since the value of an edge is I[e,1]+2I[e,2]. A complement x→1−x is an affine change over either field and simply moves a constant to the right. Therefore exhaustive scope processing finds the global contradiction on every nonzero-total member, in polynomial search work for this fixed scope size. The checked row combination need not equal a hand-picked sum of one row per vertex.

## Why the field matters, even if all bounded scopes can be chosen freely

For one ternary star, normalize edge signs/translations so feasible triples have the form (a,b,a+b+c). The contribution of one block to any linear Boolean-indicator form is an arbitrary unary function f(t). A constant sum f(a)+g(b)+h(a+b+c) forces each first difference h(z+1)−h(z) to be constant as z varies (hold a fixed, compare a and a+1, vary b). This difference must be annihilated by 3. In characteristic other than three it is zero, so f,g,h must each be constant: the only equations are block sums. In characteristic three there is one additional slope, the divergence equation up to scaling and block sums. Thus the equation spaces have dimensions 3 and 4 respectively in the nine Boolean indicator coordinates. The single-bit binary star has dimensions 0 and 1 in the wrong and matching characteristics. The experiment separately calculates these dimensions from points over F2, F3, and F5.

The following is stronger than testing the implemented candidate scopes. Suppose deleting any two edges leaves the graph connected, as is the case for cubic prisms with cycle length k≥3. In a prism, if at most one deleted edge is on each cycle the cycles remain connected and at least one rung survives; if two deletions are on the same cycle, all its vertices still have rungs to the intact other cycle.

Take a flow over F_p (p=2 or 3), an **inconsistent** total charge, and any Boolean scope on at most w coordinates, where w<|E|. Some edge is untouched, and a vertex at its endpoint cannot have any of its cross-star clauses contained in the scope: every such clause uses a bit on that edge. Repair the total charge at that vertex only. The repaired global model set satisfies every original clause in the scope. It is a nonempty affine flow coset over F_p. Every two edge states are jointly uniform on this coset: prescribe the pair arbitrarily, subtract its incidence contribution, and solve on the still-connected graph after deleting those edges. The fibers have the same size by linearity, a power of p.

Over a field of different characteristic each such fiber size is invertible. If a local affine equation follows from the original clauses in the scope, it holds on this whole repaired coset. Write its left side as a sum of unary functions of edge states (including the zero functions on unused edges). Average the equation over the fiber obtained by fixing one arbitrary edge value. Every other edge is still uniform. Only the fixed edge's unary contribution could change; the right side is constant, hence this contribution is independent of that edge's state. This holds for every edge. It follows that the equation is a linear combination of one-hot block sums, or is trivial in the single-bit F2 encoding. Changing the repaired vertex by scope causes no inconsistency: **all** rows thus derived hold on every independent assignment of edge states. No number of these wrong-characteristic rows can refute the input by Gaussian elimination. This is unconditional but specifically about local affine consequences from raw clauses and linear combinations, not nonlinear reasoning, arbitrary new extension variables, or all SAT algorithms.

## Three shared Boolean ports

For the [mixed-field experiments](../experiments/mixed_modular.py), give two independent prisms the same three port bits. Subdivide three separate rungs of each prism by a new degree-two vertex; its divergence must equal the corresponding port bit. This is a three-argument relation (two incident edge states and one Boolean), still encoded by width-at-most-three clauses. Put charge −r at one original vertex, zero at the others, and no other common variables. The bank over F_p extends a port word exactly when its *integer* Hamming weight equals r modulo p; a spanning-tree solve gives an explicit extension. Thus requiring odd weight in F2 and weight two modulo 3 admits no three-bit word; changing the F3 demand to one gives three actual single-bit port choices with checked joint lifts. Each individual bank is feasible. This is a true shared-variable conjunction, not a disjoint union of an already-UNSAT formula with irrelevant clauses.

One field alone cannot certify this mixed conflict with the above bounded-scope rule for sufficiently large prisms. Fix a port word feasible for the *learning* field's bank, and an actual extension in that bank. For the other bank fix the same ports and repair the charge at an original cubic vertex untouched by the scope (at most 2w/3 original cubic vertices have a scoped bit on all three incident edges, so |V_original|>2w/3 suffices). With these fixed ports, suppress the three new degree-two vertices: the two edges on a subdivided rung carry a single original trit/bit and its fixed translate. Any linear form is still a sum of unary functions of original edge states after this substitution; the repaired original cubic graph again has pairwise uniform edge states. Averaging as above implies the row holds on *every* choice of original edge values extended with those translations, including one fixed choice independent of the scope. This gives a common Boolean word for all learned rows, although it need not satisfy the wrong-field raw CNF. Detecting the global conflict requires reasoning beyond row elimination in just one field.

## Reproduction

[Tests](../tests/test_ternary_flow.py) check local raw words, independent finite edge-pair counts and all port valuations. [Run](../experiments/ternary_flow.py) or inspect the [record](../results/ternary_flow.json): 32 field runs on signed-scrambled charge twins, plus a Petersen graph using the generic edge constructor and 4,608 raw Boolean oracle assignments on tiny graphs. On the largest 256-vertex F3 prism the 1,152-bit, 6,144-clause imbalance produces a checked 377-term F3 certificate; F2 returns UNKNOWN. These are counts of certified local scope terms, not time units. The matching-field positive twin is satisfiable by its separately verified raw word. No general bound for unrestricted SAT has been obtained.
