# A linear-size 3-CNF whose restrictions admit no pairwise direct witness transport

For each k≥1 there is a connected unsatisfiable input with **17k+28 variables and 40k+58 clauses** (width at most three). Fix its k selector variables first. At **every** depth d≤k the resulting 2^d raw 3-CNFs are mutually incomparable under the following sufficient certificate: substitute a constant or signed source literal for each target variable, and justify every pulled-back target clause by truth, tautology, or inclusion of **one** original source clause. Even unlimited search for certificates of this type cannot prune two of these branches by certifying either direction. This is a statement about pairwise direct transport, not the width or time of every solver, every variable order, or even every combination of the lab's existing methods.

In particular the family is *easy*: a second checked proof, using the fact that both settings of a selector conserve the same parity, refutes the root without any branching. It is essential not to confuse an obstruction to one certificate with a lower bound on deciding these instances. ## Why constant assignments and even noninjective substitutions cannot help

Call a CNF clause-minimal UNSAT if removing any one clause makes it satisfiable. Let S be clause-minimal UNSAT and T unsatisfiable. Suppose they have equal clause count, equal *total* literal count, and equally many variables actually used. Write clauses as sets of distinct literals, with no tautological clauses. A direct transport certificate S→T as above would have to be a signed isomorphism on their active variables:

1. For each target clause not sent to true or a tautology, choose one source clause that justifies its pullback. The chosen source clauses already imply satisfiability of T **if their conjunction has any model**: map that model using the substitution. Since T is UNSAT, the chosen conjunction is UNSAT and must use *every* clause of S by minimality.
2. Equal clause counts force a bijection: nothing sent to true or tautological is available to pay for a source clause. In each matched pair the target width is at least the size of the nonconstant normalized pullback, which is at least the matched source width. Summing gives equality throughout. Thus no literal occurrence was removed by constant substitution, repetition or subsumption; each pullback is literally its matched clause.
3. Every source variable occurs in some matched clause, hence in the image of some active target variable. Equal active counts imply a bijection, up to sign. Extra declared variables absent from the formulas cannot evade this argument.

This lemma is syntactic. Semantically any map vacuously transports all solutions of an UNSAT source. Proving that vacuity is precisely the information a raw clause-subsumption certificate does not have. Conversely a known model of a satisfiable target gives a constant certificate from *any* source. A hard-looking SAT target alone could not give this no-certificate theorem.

## Two formulas for each selector bit, with no change in counts

Start with a cycle of r=2k+2 cubic vertices. Pair opposite vertices by matching edges (when k=1 the skeleton is K4). Keep every matching edge length one. Two adjacent, fixed cycle edges have lengths 6 and 7; in the direction going from the length-6 edge across the length-7 edge, the remaining cycle edges occur in k ordered pairs. Depending on tag t_i, pair i has lengths (2,5) or (3,4). Each pair uses the same five degree-two vertices: allocate one or two of them to the first path and the rest to the second. The number and degrees of graph vertices do not change.

Give each vertex its own Boolean variable for each of its incident *ports*. At every vertex impose parity of its two or three ports, using the full CNF of bad local assignments. Charge one cubic vertex odd and all the others even. A wire between ports u,v of different vertices requires u=v, encoded by two binary clauses. The change of path lengths in one tagged pair changes **three** wires in each setting. Explicitly, moving one interior vertex from one path to the other reconnects the ends of three links while leaving all untouched wires common. Store common wire clauses unguarded. For the other three wires from each setting, add that setting's disabling tag literal to each of its two clauses. These clauses are ternary. Each of the six alternative unordered wire pairs is different and appears at only this selector.

The port count is 3(2k+2)+2(5k+11)=16k+28. Add k selector bits. In a completion there are 18k+30 vertex clauses and 16k+28 wire clauses. Unfixing one tag replaces six binary clauses by twelve ternary clauses. Thus at depth d every restriction has 40k+58−6d clauses, the same total number of literal occurrences, and all ports plus all unfixed tags are active. There are no duplicate clauses, repeated literals, or tautologies; also no raw clause contains another, since unrelated stars/wires use distinct ports and unguarded and guarded wire pairs are disjoint.

## Why each raw restriction is clause-minimal UNSAT

After all tags are fixed, the CNF is the odd-charge Tseitin formula on the selected graph with each wire subdivided once: a port is now an edge variable, the extra degree-two vertex is its equality constraint. The graph is 2-connected. The original spanning cycle survives deleting any one cubic vertex as a spanning path; adding the matching edges and subdividing edges preserves 2-connectivity. The sum of all vertex equations is 0=1, so there is no satisfying assignment.

For minimality, remove a clause at a single vertex v. Fix every incident edge to the unique local bad pattern excluded by that clause. This flips the parity at v, so the remaining charge demand on G−v has even total. Every even demand is realized on a connected graph: take a spanning tree, assign nontree variables zero and propagate the required tree bits from leaves to the root. Therefore all other vertex equations hold, as do all other clauses at v. This is the standard Tseitin minimality fact (see [SAT 2022, article 6, Lemma 2.18 and references](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SAT.2022.6)); the selector construction and transport conclusion require the additional argument below.

Every *partial* tag restriction is UNSAT because every completion is. For any one of its clauses choose values for the remaining tags that activate the clause (at most one tag occurs in it). In that completion it specializes to a unique original clause of the completed formula; no other surviving clause specializes to the same clause. The completed clause has a critical assignment by the previous paragraph. That assignment together with the chosen tags satisfies every clause of the partial restriction except the specified clause. Thus the partial restriction is minimal too.

## Why even signed isomorphism cannot identify distinct prefixes

In a completion group clauses by their **unsigned supports**. The groups are exactly the vertex stars of the fully subdivided graph. Every variable belongs to exactly two groups, recovering the edge between its two star vertices. Suppress degree-two vertices but retain path lengths: the junctions have degree three, matching paths have length 2, and cycle path lengths are twice the lengths in the construction. Thus the cycle is distinguished from the matching, and the two adjacent unique sentinels of lengths 12 and 14 fix its orientation and starting point. Reading (4,10) or (6,8) recovers every tag in order. Renaming variables, flipping any signs, or reordering clauses cannot change the word so recovered.

Before all tags are set, an unfixed tag belongs to **six** distinct support groups (its six possible unequal wire pairs); a port belongs to at most three (its original vertex and at most one wire per tag setting). An isomorphism must therefore map unfixed tags to unfixed tags. Completing those tags on both sides under the purported signed bijection would give isomorphic completed formulas. Yet the words that complete two *different* fixed prefixes are disjoint and the full word is an unsigned isomorphism invariant. Hence no signed isomorphism exists, and the minimality lemma rules out all direct transports at that layer. This reasoning also works if a common subset other than an initial segment of tags is fixed; it is not a statement about orders interleaving arbitrary port bits.

The family uses O(k) clauses/variables or O(k log(k+1)) bits under ordinary binary literal-index encoding. The lower bound is exactly 2^k in the number of tag positions, superpolynomial in that encoding length. Do not infer a bound of 2^{Ω(L)} in the encoded bit length L from this statement.

## A polynomial root certificate; finite controls

The two alternative triples of changed wires match **the same six ports**. In either slice, XORing their three equalities gives parity zero on that same six-port boundary. Add these common consequences, the unguarded wire equalities and the original vertex parity equations: every port appears twice, but the single odd charge remains. This yields 0=1. The implementation discovers matching equality pairs on possible guards directly from signed clauses (no metadata, charge labels or graph recognition), then tries precisely this sum. An independent replayer checks the raw local parity clauses, the equality clauses under each guard value, equality of the resulting two masks and the final XOR contradiction. Missing proofs return no conclusion. This narrow procedure is a positive control, not a complete SAT solver. Fully completed instances can also be solved by plain Gaussian elimination.

The experiment checks every single-clause critical assignment at every prefix through k=3 directly against its raw CNF (3,654 witnesses). It decodes all 62 completed graph structures and checks disjoint possible words for all 119 prefix restrictions through k=5. Independent tests scramble variable labels/signs and clause order, check a satisfiable even-charge sibling, reject invalid conservation proofs, and compare a small guard proof against full truth tables. At k=128 the root proof was run on the actual 2,204-variable, 5,178-clause formula; the 2^128 incomparable completed restrictions follow from the proof and were not enumerated.
