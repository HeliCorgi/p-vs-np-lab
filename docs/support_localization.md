# Why a high detecting digit cannot help on the phase-16 expanding family

The [phase-17 simulation](feedback_simulation.md) left open whether narrow
resolution learning could expose small integer laws whose global sum gives
a contradiction visible only in a high prime-power digit. **On the expanding
proper 3-CNF family constructed in phase 16, no such laws exist.** This
removes the dependence on the modulus for that family. It does not remove it
from the simulation theorem for arbitrary CNFs.

The issue is simultaneous localization. A learned clause can have many
ancestors in its displayed proof. We show that *all* learned clauses on
one prospective integer scope are semantically implied by one small set
of original clauses. The models of just that set already prevent every
nontrivial integer affine law on the scope.

## 1. Statement and evidence level

Let $F$ be a finite CNF in which every original clause has exactly three
distinct variables and is not a tautology. In its clause-variable incidence
graph, $N(U)$ denotes the variables occurring in the clause set $U$ and
$\partial U$ those occurring in exactly **one clause** of $U$. Suppose that
for an integer $s\ge 1$ and real $\delta>0$,

\[
              |\partial U|\ge\delta |U|
              \qquad(1\le |U|\le s).                         \tag{1}
\]

Here the bound is on the number of **clauses**, not the number of variables.
Let $w,b$ be nonnegative integer budgets satisfying

\[
        2\lfloor w/\delta\rfloor\le s,\qquad
        2\lfloor b/\delta\rfloor\le s.                       \tag{2}
\]

**Theorem.** Permit any length of ordinary resolution and weakening on the
original coordinates, provided all used proof lines have width at most
$w$. Fix any variable set $S$ with $|S|\le b$. Take any collection of
the resulting clauses supported on $S$, as well as any original clauses
supported on $S$. Let $X\subseteq\{0,1\}^{S}$ be their simultaneous local
model set. Then:

1. There is a single $K(S)\subseteq F$ with
   $|K(S)|\le\lfloor |S|/\delta\rfloor$ that entails **all** those clauses.
   The same $K(S)$ works for every choice of proofs.
2. For any choice of up to two coordinates of $S$, $X$ contains a full
   coordinate face on those coordinates: the other coordinates can be
   held fixed across all values of the chosen ones. The fixed values can
   differ between choices of face.
3. The affine integer lattice generated by $X$ is $\mathbb Z^S$.
   Every affine equality or congruence valid on $X$ holds on *every*
   integer vector on $S$. This includes moduli with arbitrarily large
   prime powers. In fact, the degree-two multilinear lift of $X$ also
   generates its full affine integer lattice.

These are paper statements with proofs below. They do not assert that this
expansion condition or the small supports can be recognized in polynomial
time. In particular (1) is not inferred from a few sampled subfamilies.

## 2. Localize each learned clause, then pool a whole scope

For a derived clause $C$ define

\[
       \mu(C)=\min\{|U|: U\subseteq F,\quad U\models C\}.
\]

Entailment is ordinary Boolean entailment on all original variables. A
minimum exists since $F\models C$. It is not necessary to compute it.
If $U$ is inclusion-minimal entailing $C$, then
**$\partial U\subseteq \operatorname{vars}(C)$.** Indeed, suppose a variable
$v\notin\operatorname{vars}(C)$ occurs only in $D\in U$. By minimality,
some assignment satisfies $U\setminus\{D\}$ and falsifies $C$. Set $v$ to
make its literal in $D$ true. Neither the other clauses nor $C$ change,
contradicting $U\models C$. For a tautological $C$ use the empty support.

Put $W=\lfloor w/\delta\rfloor$. We induct along a width-$w$ derivation to
show that every minimum support has size at most $W$. For an input axiom its
size is at most one, hence at most $s$. For a resolution step, the union
of two minimum parent supports entails the resolvent by soundness. Its size
is at most $2W\le s$, so a minimum support of the *child* also has size at
most $s$. For weakening, use the parent support alone. In each case, (1) and
the inclusion of boundaries above now give

\[
         \delta\mu(C)\le |\partial U|\le |\operatorname{vars}(C)|\le w
\]

for a nonempty minimum support $U$. This proves the inductive bound;
size zero needs no expansion hypothesis. In particular an empty refuting
line cannot be derived within these budgets, since its minimum would
then have size zero, whereas the empty set of clauses does not entail
the empty clause. A raw axiom cited directly in a leaf need not have been
computed by this prefix: the singleton containing it already has size
at most $s$ and boundary in its own variable set.

Now fix $S$. Define the *common support*

\[
     K(S)=\bigcup\{U\subseteq F:\ |U|\le s,\quad \partial U\subseteq S\}.
\]

Every set inside this union has size at most $B_S=\lfloor |S|/\delta\rfloor$
by (1). The crucial point is that the union has that same bound, not a
bound multiplied by the number of learned clauses:

- For any $U,V$, $\partial(U\cup V)\subseteq\partial U\cup\partial V$.
  A variable unique in the union occurs in a clause of at least one of
  the sets, and in no other clause of that set.
- If $U,V$ are members of the displayed family, their union has size at
  most $2B_S\le s$ and boundary in $S$. It is therefore another member,
  of size at most $B_S$. The argument also covers $B_S=0$.
- Induction over this finite family gives $|K(S)|\le B_S$ and
  $\partial K(S)\subseteq S$.

Every minimum support of every width-$w$ consequence $C$ on $S$ that
was obtained by the specified derivation has size at most $s$ and
boundary in $S$, hence is contained in $K(S)$. A directly cited raw
axiom on $S$ also belongs to $K(S)$. Consequently every model of
$K(S)$, restricted to $S$, lies in $X$. This proves part 1. We do **not**
assert that $K(S)$ contains all ancestors of $C$ in its given proof,
that all logical consequences of an UNSAT $F$ are narrow-derivable,
or that the sets of models are equal.

## 3. Match clauses to variables outside the selected coordinates

We first deduce the Hall surplus needed here from (1), without a second
expansion postulate. A nonempty subfamily $T$ of at most $s$ proper
ternary clauses has a private variable by (1). Removing the one clause
containing it decreases the size of its neighborhood by at least one.
Repeat until one clause remains, whose neighborhood has size three.
Hence $|N(T)|\ge |T|+2$. This argument uses the proper three-variable
axioms, not properness of the derived clauses.

Choose $J\subseteq S$ with $|J|\le2$. In the incidence graph on $K(S)$
forbid the right vertices in $J$. Every nonempty $T\subseteq K(S)$ has
$|T|\le s$ and

\[
        |N(T)\setminus J|\ge |T|+2-|J|\ge |T|.
\]

By [Hall's theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-10.37.26) there is
a matching of all these clauses to *distinct* variables outside $J$.
Set each matched variable to satisfy its literal in its assigned clause.
All clauses of $K(S)$ are now true whatever values $J$ takes. Fix the
other variables arbitrarily. The $2^{|J|}$ resulting assignments, when
restricted to $S$, give the desired face in $X$. If $K(S)$ is empty
the empty matching suffices; this includes the empty-scope case. For
general non-ternary CNFs a separately verified one-variable Hall surplus
would suffice for the affine conclusion; it should not be inferred from
a last clause of width one or two.

For each $v\in S$, subtract the two local models supplied by $J=\{v\}$.
The difference is the coordinate basis vector $e_v$. The lattice of model
differences therefore contains every basis vector, and a nonempty set
with these differences has full affine integer lattice. Equivalently, if
$a\cdot x=r$ for every local word, the unit differences force every
$a_v=0$ and then $r=0$. For a congruence modulo $d\ge 2$, they show
$d\mid a_v$ for each $v$ and $d\mid r$. Thus the same row holds at every
integer vector.

**Quadratic corollary.** Map $x$ to its single coordinates and all squarefree
pairs $(x_v,\,x_i x_j)_{v\in S,\,i<j}$. For any pair $i,j$, take the signed
four-corner difference of its lifted coordinate face: value at $11$ minus
those at $10,01$ plus that at $00$. Its only nonzero component is the pair
coordinate $ij$, where it equals one. This difference is an integer sum of
point differences. A single-coordinate flip in the lift gives one in the
linear coordinate $i$ and possible entries in pair coordinates; subtract
the already obtained pair basis vectors with their coefficients to isolate
the pure linear $e_i$. This gives the full difference lattice in the lifted
space. So even local multilinear quadratic integer **equalities or
congruences** must be identities on these coordinates. Boolean squares are
normalized by $x_i^2=x_i$; this is not a statement about inequalities or
all nonlinear consequences. Degree three already admits the nonzero
clause-falsity polynomial of any original proper ternary clause.

## 4. Consequence for the high-digit proposal

In the [phase-17 certificate](feedback_simulation.md#1-rules-and-the-simple-field-reduction)
each leaf is checked against the local models of its cited learned or
raw clauses, all on its own scope. By part 3 every accepted leaf is
satisfied by the restriction of **any** global integer vector. Pick, for
example, the same all-zero vector at every leaf. All rows hold
simultaneously, even if their local Boolean witness assignments are
mutually incompatible. A sound weighted sum of these rows cannot
contradict that integer vector. No bound on the number of leaves, modulus,
integer coefficient bit lengths, or detecting digit is used. In particular,
choosing a different modulus at each leaf is no escape.

The [phase-16 proper linear family](correlation_proof_boundary.md#4-a-scalable-control-in-the-same-proper-linear-3-cnf-class)
has (1) with $\delta=1/2$ and $s=\lfloor 10^{-16}n\rfloor$ for
sufficiently large $n$, and has an UNSAT signing on the same supports.
Its ordinary PC degree is $\Omega(n)$ in every characteristic using
[Mikša–Nordström's boundary-expansion criterion, Theorem 4.2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol033-ccc2015/LIPIcs.CCC.2015.467/LIPIcs.CCC.2015.467.pdf).
For this family (2) is simply $4w\le s$ and $4b\le s$. Hence any
refutation of the stated hybrid form has $\max(w,b)>s/4$, **even with
unbounded detecting digit**. The proof of this last conclusion uses
the expansion directly, not a converse to a PC simulation. The
constant is not offered as a practical benchmark size.

This rules out a positive answer to the prior specific target *on this
family*. The high-digit gate for a different all-field-hard family is
still open. It remains possible to use a different Boolean encoding,
introduce extended coordinates, use genuine inequalities, or use a
different proof language. Fixed assignments require a separate analysis;
see [the conditional result](conditional_localization.md). A lower bound
on these two proof resources does not bound all SAT algorithms, and
does not by itself imply an exponential lower bound on certificate
length.

## 5. Prior work and what was added

The minimum-entailing-subformula measure and its binary subadditivity/
private-variable width argument are classical:
[Ben-Sasson and Wigderson, *Short Proofs Are Narrow*, §5](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/emo/SatSem05/Papers/BensassonWidgerson01.pdf).
The pooled support is likewise an established construction, not a new
lower-bound method. In the more general clustered framework of
[Mikša and Nordström, JACM 71(6), 2024, Definition 4.16 and Lemmas 4.17–4.18](https://jakobnordstrom.se/docs/publications/GeneralizedMethodPCdegree_JACM.pdf),
the union of small sets with the required boundary localization stays small
and entails the narrow clause. Ordinary clause-variable incidence suffices
here; the elementary argument was included to expose the budgets.

The deduction for this project is to pool **all clauses potentially cited
by a local integer row**, use the simultaneous free faces of the resulting
raw fragment, and transfer their integral difference lattice to the entire
weighted-sum certificate. It closes a specific loophole left by the
previous degree simulation. No research-priority or P versus NP solution
claim is made.
