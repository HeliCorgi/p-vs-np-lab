# New main question: can an unrestricted SAT search keep polynomially many live branches?

**Outcome.** We have an exact decision-only pruning algorithm, and proofs both of where a first label saves an entire exponential layer and of where it fails. We do not have a polynomial-time algorithm deciding all inputs. The next milestone is an all-input lemma about a particular polynomial-time label. A new encoding, an isolated contradiction, or a better small-instance score does not meet that milestone.

## Research priority, not a probability estimate

As a route to the original unrestricted problem, we rate the previous few-parity-conflict program **2/10**, the decision-only bounded-width research question **4/10**, and the claim that the *particular tested driver/order* runs in polynomial time **ruled out**. These are qualitative judgments: the old program loses information even if it tries every proper linear observation, whereas the new program targets a sufficient condition on the original input and produces useful kill tests at modest cost. The jump from 2 to 4 reflects a sharper target, not evidence that an efficient universal label exists. The positive and negative width results are about our specified key/order. A matching or SCC algorithm may solve the controls by other means.

## A sufficient condition measured on the original input

Fix a signed 3-CNF F of encoded length L and an explicitly listed variable order. After fixing a prefix of that order, each residual is a restriction of the original clauses and still has polynomial length. A list means that **at least one** listed residual must be satisfiable. Duplicated formulas need not be retained. Splitting every member on the next bit preserves that disjunction; distinct prefixes form a binary tree regardless of the order.

Seek a single uniform deterministic procedure which, for two such residuals H and A at one depth, computes a string lambda(F,H,A) in time poly(L). The string describes a property of the decision question SAT(H or A); it need not encode any witness, count, or truth table. It may use the original F. Two obligations suffice:

1. **Safe equality.** For a fixed F, depth, and anchor H, equal strings for partners A and B imply that SAT(H or A) and SAT(H or B) have the same truth value.
2. **Few strings at a satisfiable anchor.** There is a fixed polynomial B(L), with a known usable upper bound, such that whenever H is satisfiable, all partner residuals at that depth produce at most B(L) different strings. For example, it suffices to prove this for every anchor and partner from the full restriction tree. There is no bound required at unsatisfiable anchors.

Here is the proof. If a frontier exceeds B(L)+1 members, designate one H and inspect B(L)+1 distinct **partners**. If two strings match, drop one of their partners: a satisfiable H protects the whole list, and when H is unsatisfiable the two partners have equal SAT status. If all those strings differ, H is impossible by obligation 2; drop H. Repeat until at most B(L)+1 remain. Each stage starts with at most twice this many members. There are at most n stages; at depth n each residual is a Boolean constant. The work is O(n (B(L)+1)^2) label comparisons/computations up to polynomial factors for storing, constructing and comparing strings/formulas. Thus the entire decision procedure runs in polynomial time in the input encoding.

This is the SAT self-reduction and collision/counting argument from [Mahaney](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(82)90002-2), particularly the short proof attributed to Agrawal in [Grochow's exposition](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2016/162/). The anchor-local sufficient condition is how we organize this experiment; no new proof of Mahaney's theorem is claimed. Conversely, if P=NP, the one-bit exact SAT decision of H or A is an efficient label meeting the obligations. The criterion thus identifies an equivalent search target, **not** evidence that such a useful procedure exists. An exact label can be enormously expensive to compute; a constant label merges SAT with UNSAT and is unsound in general.

## What our implementation actually does

[decision_labels.py](../src/pnp_lab/decision_labels.py) constructs the disjunction of two residual CNFs by distributing OR over AND, at most the product of their original clause counts, with width at most six. These big clauses are **temporary**: the next frontier continues to use raw restrictions of original clauses, so repeated disjunctions never cause iterated blow-up inside one label. Empty CNF is true, empty clause is false. Tautologies, duplicates and subsumed clauses are removed exactly.

The syntax mode uses normalized CNF itself. The affine mode groups clauses by their variable support. If all false patterns of one parity occur on a support, precisely those clauses are equivalent to one XOR equation; additional same-support clauses remain. Once the nonlinear clauses K are separated, variables absent from K are existentially eliminated from the XOR system before forming a label: Gaussian elimination pivots on private columns first, then canonically row-reduces the shared equations. Every assignment to shared bits that satisfies the remaining rows lifts by solving the private pivots in ascending order. Equal (K, remaining rows) thus guarantees equal *existence*. No need to count or recover auxiliary completions. The code compares entire immutable keys, so a hash collision cannot justify an incorrect deletion. Grouping width and input bit costs here are polynomial; the number of distinct keys need not be.

A third mode, blocked, applies [blocked-clause elimination](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/mjarvisa/papers/jarvisalo-biere-heule.tacas10.pdf) before extracting parity constraints. Its repair argument, successful control and different exponential-width family are in [the stronger test](blocked_pruning.md). None of the simplifiers assumes a bound on the number of opaque positive keys.

The [Lean module](../Formal/DecisionPruning.lean) checks five semantic lemmas, including deletion and iterated splits. Its distinct-label lemma assumes an explicit bound on a common collection of positive labels; that is a sufficient special case of the anchor-local statement above. No polynomial bound for the implementation or runtime theorem is asserted in Lean.

The [driver](../src/pnp_lab/decision_pruning.py) takes a safe merge when available. A proved-true label settles the list as SAT, and a proved-false pair permits deleting both members. If the user sets a width limit, the driver may return UNKNOWN. It **never** treats that numeric limit as a proof of obligation 2. With no limit it decides SAT exactly but has no polynomial time guarantee. It returns a decision, not a purported source witness. The standard SAT decision-to-search reduction would make at most n additional decisions in the event of an all-input polynomial bound.

## What to try and when to drop a proposal

* [Parity shadows](parity_shadow.md) close out the previous main question. There are explicit matched SAT/UNSAT examples which agree on **all** lower-rank projected parity-feasibility questions, even when the linear combinations are chosen adaptively. Matching structure in the clauses distinguishes them. This refutes the query model, not arbitrary reasoning from those clauses.
* [Controls for counting and 2-CNF covers](pruning_obstructions.md) reject two convenient proxies for a decision label. An easy comparator forces exponentially many anchored model counts, and a fixed modulus can conflate zero with a positive count. Exact relation covers by projected 2-CNFs can be exponentially long even for independent satisfiable clauses.
* [Tests of the actual affine codec](codec_controls.md) identify which restricted variables it really forgets and which cause an exponential label set. This is a limit on a polynomial-time, count-blind implementation, not merely on a hypothetical exact model counter.
* [Alternation-trading replay](alternation_trade.md) is a negative-direction calibration, not a second main research program: the lower bounds concern a restricted space budget and a restricted proof system. Even a fixed superlinear exponent is not a separation from all of P.

**Next decision point:** the first private-marker example is already repaired by blocked-clause elimination. The implication triangles in the stronger example can themselves be contracted using equivalence of literals. Defeating one more representation alone is too weak a milestone. A candidate worth investigating is *one-way witness transport*: to delete A in favor of B it suffices to give a polynomially checkable way to turn every A-model into a B-model. Start with signed literal substitutions for which every pulled-back target clause is tautological or subsumed by a source clause; require mappings to be found in polynomial time, not supplied by an oracle. Test families with coupled nonlinear interfaces as well as the known equality padding. To justify a claim about P=NP the rule (or another method) still needs a uniform polynomial bound on surviving branches or positive-label diversity. Otherwise report its explicit failure family.
