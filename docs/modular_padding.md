# Hiding all small-scope affine equations and recovering them by sequential elimination

Even the **correct characteristic** need not help when the Boolean representation is changed. The [construction and checked projector](../src/pnp_lab/modular_padding.py) have a positive and a negative control. No step assumes the encoded formula came from a graph.

## A private chain that erases all bounded-scope linear consequences

For each proper clause C = L OR b of width 2 or 3 (L is a nonempty list of literals; the variables in C are distinct), allocate t new private bits and replace C by

    (L OR h₁), (¬h₁ OR h₂), ..., (¬h_{t−1} OR h_t), (¬h_t OR b).

Helpers occur in exactly this clause's chain and nowhere else. Projecting helpers gives back C: if L and b are false the chain forces h₁ and then h_t true, contradicting the last clause. Otherwise choose all helpers equal to the Boolean value of b. This gives decision equivalence and a deterministic lift; extra private assignments generally break equality of model counts.

Fix any Boolean scope S of size ≤w and take all raw clauses completely supported on S, or any subset of them. If t>w, some private helper in **every** chain is missing from S. Then:

1. An arbitrary assignment of original bits extends to satisfy these scoped rows. In the portion before a missing helper set helpers true; in the portion after it set them false. The missing helper breaks both incident links, so neither end can force an incompatible value at the other end. Different chains have private helpers. A missing endpoint helper only makes this simpler.
2. For any particular helper in S there are two scoped models differing *only at that helper*. Choose the original bits so both L and b of its chain are true; this is possible since their variables are disjoint and L is nonempty. Make helpers earlier in that chain zero, later ones one. Either value of the chosen helper satisfies even all implications on that chain. For all other chains extend the same originals by part 1. This construction is only for the scoped rows; no assumption that the full input is satisfiable is used.

Subtracting these two models kills the coefficient of every helper in any entailed affine equation on S, in **every** field. With those coefficients gone, part 1 kills all original coefficients and the constant term too. The local affine hull is the **entire** coordinate space. This is a statement about all possible w-coordinate pools and all locally sound linear consequences, not just a failed scope heuristic. It does not rule out using the actual nonlinear clauses or deriving wider intermediate statements. Independent signed negation/relabeling preserves affine dimension.

## A generic polynomial repair

In the current active CNF find a variable occurring in exactly one clause C₊ positively and exactly one clause C₋ negatively. On pivot v these clauses are (v OR A),(¬v OR B). Replace both with A OR B if its width is at most three; if the resolvent is tautological simply drop both. Ignore a non-tautological wider candidate. This is a restricted Davis–Putnam elimination step (see [Davis–Putnam, 1960](https://doi.org/10.1145/321033.321034)), applied **successively**, so later steps can use earlier resolvents. It is not full resolution saturation. Each step eliminates one variable and reduces the number of active clauses by at least one. The finder indexes occurrences and chooses a deterministically ordered available pivot. The number of eliminations and the total discovery/checking work are polynomial in the explicit signed-clause input; the width-three cap bounds a single newly stored row. The following [modular hull](modular_hull.md) also has polynomial cost at its fixed scope width.

The replayer normalizes initial clauses into sets, ignores tautologies, and maintains an active clause table with stable IDs and separate occurrence counts. Each certificate entry specifies a pivot and two live IDs; the checker verifies the signed privacy condition, forms their actual resolvent, checks the width policy, and assigns the new ID after the previous steps. Finally it replays the inner modular proof against the **reconstructed** residual. A single-done resolution pass over the initial rows would not support these long chains. The solver's dimensions and statistics do not enter the checker. Absence of a contradiction returns UNKNOWN.

To lift a satisfying residual assignment, process the steps backwards. If A is false set v true, otherwise set v false. If A is false, B is true by the resolvent, also when it was tautological. No other clause at that step contains v. The implemented lift independently checks the final word against every original raw clause.

On the ternary one-hot graph source each indicator appears once positively in the positive domain clause, and at least twice negatively in its binary exclusions. Therefore no original variable is eligible; complementation just swaps the counts. Every fresh bit remains eligible until it is contracted. After eliminating any one helper, the resulting clause reconnects its two neighbors or connects one neighbor to an endpoint. Before the last elimination within a chain every intermediate endpoint or link has width ≤3; the last gives exactly the original proper clause, also width ≤3. Other chains are untouched. There are exactly mt eliminations for m original clauses, in any order, before the original signed clause set is recovered on its named coordinates. Counts of original occurrences did not change. Gaussian elimination of the rediscovered native-field equations now works exactly as on the [unmasked inputs](ternary_flow.md).

## An NP-complete presentation where even this combined pipeline is silent

For **each** bit v of the padded CNF (including private bits and any originals), add two fresh aliases u,w and the six binary implications between every ordered pair of different vertices of (v,u,w). These clauses force all three equal and impose no restriction on v. Every bit now has at least two positive and two negative occurrences. The private-pivot pass cannot take even one step.

A scope R of ≤w protected bits maps under the identification (v,u,w)↦v to a scope R′ of at most w bits of the unguarded chains. The six alias implications become tautologies and regular clauses remain regular clauses. Any locally justified affine row of the protected input therefore maps to an affine row valid on the scoped unguarded models on R′. By the full-hull argument it becomes an identity. Hence every learned row holds on *every assignment obeying the aliases*, in any characteristic, regardless of which original clauses this assignment falsifies. In particular even taking the Boolean combination of all fields' **learned affine rows alone** cannot yield a contradiction. This does not say the learned equalities are useless for simplifying the raw clauses: a separate signed equivalence contraction can remove the alias guards. We have not proved a bound for any system that feeds such consequences back into the original nonlinear representation.

For fixed w and t=w+1 this is a polynomial-size decision reduction from arbitrary proper 3-SAT, with width at most three throughout. Restricting to exactly-three distinct variables per source clause does not lose NP-hardness: shorter normalized clauses can be padded by fresh dummy variables using both signs in all combinations (a width-two clause gives 2 width-three clauses; a unit gives 4), and a false empty clause can be replaced by all eight signed clauses on three new variables. SAT on the guarded images is therefore NP-complete, **not** proven outside P. The specific frontend's failure is unconditional, not a worst-case bound on all solvers.

## Reproduction

The [experiment](../results/modular_padding.json) uses arbitrary sign flips and permutations and length ten (default learner scope width nine). On the 3,912-variable, 4,224-clause unbalanced instance no nonzero local row was discovered before peeling. Exactly 3,840 successive eliminations recovered 72 active variables and 384 clauses, and the resulting raw-bound contradiction was rechecked. The matching balanced sibling's restored assignment satisfies the original CNF even after initially erasing all private helper values. With guards on the smallest length-ten instance (4,401 variables, 10,386 clauses), both the SAT and UNSAT examples returned UNKNOWN in both coefficient fields. The learned rank was exactly 2,934, the rank of the alias equalities. The [tests](../tests/test_modular_padding.py) also exercise malformed proofs, tautological resolvents, empty and duplicate rows, exhaustive small semantic checks and SAT witness lifting. These are finite controls; the general scope obstruction above comes from the proof rather than the benchmark sizes.
