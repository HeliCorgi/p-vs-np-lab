# Large separated monomial spaces from boundary expansion

This note supplies a dimension statement, rather than only a refutation-degree
statement, for the expanding proper linear 3-CNFs used in phase 16. Its algebraic
input is the published graph-reduction operator of Mikša and Nordström. The
counting and bounded-occurrence arguments below are written out with finite
parameters. They do not assume the recent Braun preprint or its Lean code.

The final section combines this space with the
[companion common-annihilator DAG criterion](affine_dag_annihilator.md).
Both pieces were subjected to internal mathematical review. The specified F2
existential exponential conclusion is now connected end to end in
[`Phase20.lean`](../formal_pc/ProofLab/Phase20.lean), including family existence;
see the [formalization record and exact scope](phase20_formalization.md).
External specialist review remains outstanding. An exponential bound for this proof system is not a
lower bound for every SAT algorithm and does not resolve P versus NP. No
claim of historical priority is made.

## 1. Setting and the separated space

Let \(F\) be a CNF on \(n\) original Boolean variables, with \(m\) clauses. Every
clause has exactly three distinct variables, and the supports of any two
distinct clauses meet in at most one variable. Every variable occurs in at
most \(\Delta\) clauses. Write \(\partial A\) for the variables occurring in
exactly one clause of \(A\). Assume

\[
 |\partial A|>|A|/2 \qquad(0<|A|\le s).                    \tag{1}
\]

For a variable set \(T\), define its pooled support

\[
 \operatorname{Sup}_s(T)=
 \bigcup\{A\subseteq F: |A|\le s,\ \partial A\subseteq T\}.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]

Let \(C_B(F)\) be the vector space of polynomials derivable by ordinary
polynomial calculus through degree \(B\), with the original clause-falsity
polynomials and the explicit Boolean axioms \(x_i^2-x_i\). This is the actual
closure under addition, scalar multiplication, and legal multiplication by
one variable. It is not just the span of degree-bounded axiom multiples.

For an integer \(k\le B\), let

\[
 \mathcal G_k=\{T\subseteq[n]: |T|=k,
                         \operatorname{Sup}_s(T)=\varnothing\},
 \qquad
 U_k=\operatorname{span}\{x_T:T\in\mathcal G_k\},
 \quad x_T=\prod_{i\in T}x_i.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

**Separated-monomial lemma.** Over every field, if \(3\le B\le s/4\), then

\[
       U_k\cap C_B(F)=\{0\},\qquad \dim U_k=|\mathcal G_k|.
                                                               \tag{4}
\]

Here and below the inequalities with real numbers are literal inequalities;
an integer \(B\) must satisfy them. The lemma applies to all signings of the
given clause supports. It does not require the signing to be unsatisfiable.

### Why the ordinary-PC conclusion follows

The algebraic input is [Mikša–Nordström, JACM 2024, Definition 4.26, Lemmas
4.20 and 4.27–4.33, and the proof of Theorem 4.11](https://jakobnordstrom.se/docs/publications/GeneralizedMethodPCdegree_JACM.pdf).
In the ordinary clause-variable graph, take one clause per left vertex, one
variable per right vertex, and no additional polynomial family \(Q\). Their
operator \(R\), on multilinear polynomials through degree \(B\), has these
properties:

\[
 R(f_C)=0,\qquad
 R(x_i t)=R(x_iR(t))\quad(\deg t<B),\qquad
 R(x_T)=x_T\quad(\operatorname{Sup}_s(T)=\varnothing).
                                                               \tag{5}
\]

The products in (5) are multilinearized. The last identity follows directly
from the definition: reduction modulo the ideal generated by an empty
support, with \(Q=\varnothing\), is the identity. Positive boundary expansion
also makes every at-most-\(s\) clause subfamily satisfiable, as required in
the construction: remove a clause with a private variable, satisfy the rest
inductively, and repair that clause using its private variable.

The paper labels zero as the true Boolean value. Apply its construction
directly to our raw polynomial base by reversing the literal labels in
that convention; the variable coordinates and all unsigned supports stay
the same. Thus the fixed monomials are exactly \(x_T\). Conjugating an
operator by \(x\mapsto1-x\) would instead give a different fixed basis and
is not the justification being used here.

There is a small multiplication detail worth making explicit. Published
Lemma 4.33 is worded for a variable not occurring in a term, whereas the
PC invariant also needs repeated-variable multiplication. If \(x\) occurs
in \(t\), put \(A=\operatorname{Sup}_s(t)\). For a term \(t'\) occurring
in \(R(t)\), put \(A'=\operatorname{Sup}_s(xt')\), with products
multilinearized. The degree-compatible reduction has \(\deg t'\le\deg t\).
When \(\deg t<B\), both support sets have size at most \(s/2\), by the
published support bound. Its normal-form neighborhood bound gives
\(N(t')\subseteq N(A)\cup\operatorname{Vars}(t)\); since
\(x\in\operatorname{Vars}(t)\), this holds for \(xt'\) as well. Therefore

\[
 \partial(A\cup A')
   \subseteq\partial A\cup(\partial A'\setminus N(A))
   \subseteq\operatorname{Vars}(t).
\]

The union has at most \(s\) clauses and is eligible in the definition of
\(\operatorname{Sup}_s(t)\), so \(A'\subseteq A\). Published Lemma 4.30
now allows every \(xt'\) to be reduced modulo the same ideal \(I(A)\).
Normal-form compatibility with multiplication gives

\[
 R(xR(t))=R_{I(A)}(xR_{I(A)}(t))
         =R_{I(A)}(xt)=R_{I(A)}(t)=R(t)=R(xt).
\]

Here \(xt=t\) in the Boolean quotient. This supplies the repeated-variable
case of (5), with the same strict precursor-degree condition. The case
where \(x\) does not occur in \(t\) is the published Lemma 4.33 argument.

Unused ambient variables are allowed. Apply the published construction to
the variables occurring in \(F\), and extend it by the identity on unused
squarefree monomials: write \(p=\sum_Y x_Y p_Y\), where \(Y\) ranges over
unused variables and \(p_Y\) uses only occurring variables, and set
\(\widetilde R(p)=\sum_Y x_Y R(p_Y)\). The extension is linear, fixes
every good monomial, and has the kernel-preservation property needed
below. For an unused variable, prove the last property directly rather
than implicitly assuming the additional identity \(R^2=R\).
If \(\widetilde R(p)=0\), linear independence of the outside monomials
implies \(R(p_Y)=0\) for each \(Y\). Multiplication and multilinearization
by an unused variable only merge some of these coefficients by addition.
Their images under \(R\) remain zero by linearity. Multiplication by a
used variable is covered coefficientwise by (5). Thus all \(n\) ambient
coordinates, including variables isolated after the deletion in section 4,
are legitimately included in the dimension count.

Let \(\mathrm{ml}\) denote Boolean multilinearization. We prove, by induction
on an **ordinary** PC derivation, that
\(R(\mathrm{ml}(p))=0\) (or \(\widetilde R(\mathrm{ml}(p))=0\) when
unused variables are present) for every derived \(p\) of derivation degree at most
\(B\). Clause inputs vanish by (5), Boolean inputs multilinearize to zero,
and linear rules preserve the invariant. In a multiplication step from a
nonzero \(p\) to \(x_i p\), ordinary polynomial degree satisfies
\(\deg(x_i p)=\deg p+1\). Hence the legal premise has degree at most \(B-1\),
and so does \(\mathrm{ml}(p)\). Linearity and (5) give

\[
 R(\mathrm{ml}(x_i p))
 =R(x_i\mathrm{ml}(p))
 =R(x_iR(\mathrm{ml}(p)))=0.
\]

With unused variables, apply this argument to each used-variable
coefficient and the preceding kernel-preservation argument to unused-variable
multiplication. Every coefficient of a legal nonzero precursor still has
degree at most \(B-1\). The zero premise is immediate. This checks the precise degree convention;
no free Boolean simplification is being added to the ordinary proof system.
Every member of \(U_k\) is multilinear and is fixed by \(R\), so (4) follows.
Distinct squarefree monomials are linearly independent over any field.

## 2. Count the obstructions to empty support

Put

\[
       q=\max(1,3\Delta),\qquad C=q^2.
                                                               \tag{6}
\]

The clause-intersection graph joins two clauses when their supports meet. Its
maximum degree is at most \(3(\Delta-1)\le q\). The number of connected
\(t\)-clause subsets is at most

\[
                         m C^{t-1}.                            \tag{7}
\]

Indeed, every connected set has a spanning tree and a traversal of that tree
starting at a clause, of length \(2(t-1)\), visiting exactly its clauses.
Choose one such traversal canonically for each set. Distinct clause sets
cannot give the same visited walk, and there are at most
\(m q^{2(t-1)}\) walks of that length.

It is enough to exclude boundaries of **connected** sets. If a nonempty
\(A\) has \(\partial A\subseteq T\), each connected component \(A_i\) of its
clause-intersection graph has \(\partial A_i\subseteq T\). Components use
disjoint variable sets. Each component still has at most \(s\) clauses.

Every nonempty \(A\) of size at most \(s\) has at least three boundary
variables. For \(t=|A|\le3\), linearity gives

\[
 |\partial A|\ge3t-2\sum_{v}\binom{\deg_A(v)}2
              \ge3t-t(t-1)=4t-t^2.
                                                               \tag{8}
\]

To justify the first inequality, a nonprivate variable of multiplicity
\(r\ge2\) contributes \(r\le2\binom r2\) incidences. The second inequality
uses that each pair of clauses shares at most one variable. Equation (8)
gives bounds \(3,4,3\) for \(t=1,2,3\). For \(t\ge4\), (1) supplies the
bound three and, more precisely,

\[
                 |\partial A|\ge\lfloor t/2\rfloor+1.
                                                               \tag{9}
\]

Let \(p\in(0,1]\). For a Bernoulli-\(p\) variable set \(T\), a specified
boundary of size \(b\) is contained in \(T\) with probability \(p^b\).
For a uniformly random \(k\)-set the same probability is at most
\((k/n)^b\), including the case \(b>k\), where it is zero. Consequently,
for either model with the corresponding \(p\), the sum of probabilities of
all connected boundary obstructions is at most

\[
 m\left[p^3+Cp^4+C^2p^3+C^3p^3+C^4p^3+
            \frac{C^5(1+C)p^4}{1-C^2p}\right]
        \le 7mC^4p^3,
 \qquad C^2p\le\tfrac12.                                    \tag{10}
\]

The geometric series bounds all \(t\ge6\), extending the finite range
\(t\le s\) only upwards. Terms for \(t=2j\) and \(t=2j+1\) have total at
most \(mC^{2j-1}(1+C)p^{j+1}\). The first five terms are at most
\(5mC^4p^3\); the tail is at most \(4mC^6p^4\le2mC^4p^3\).

**Uniform-set consequence.** If \(C^2k/n\le1/2\), then

\[
 |\mathcal G_k|\ge
 \left(1-7mC^4(k/n)^3\right)\binom nk.                         \tag{11}
\]

For example, \(14mC^4k^3\le n^3\) makes at least half of all degree-\(k\)
squarefree monomials available. This elementary union bound already permits
\(k=\Theta(n^{2/3})\) when \(m=O(n)\), with fixed occurrence bound. The next
argument permits a small **linear** \(k\).

## 3. Positive association gives an exponential counting bound

For each connected nonempty \(A\), the event
\(E_A=\{\partial A\not\subseteq T\}\) is decreasing in the coordinate bits
of a Bernoulli random set. Such events are positively associated:

\[
                    \Pr\left[\bigcap_A E_A\right]
                      \ge\prod_A\Pr[E_A].                    \tag{12}
\]

For completeness, the two-function version follows by induction on the
number of independent bits. Condition on the last bit. The induction
hypothesis bounds each conditional expectation of the product by the
product of the conditional expectations. Those two expectations are both
decreasing functions of the last bit, so their covariance is
\(p(1-p)(a_1-a_0)(b_1-b_0)\ge0\). Apply this to event indicators and then
iterate; products of decreasing event indicators remain decreasing. Thus
(12) uses no independence assumption between the different boundaries.

By (10), (12), and \(\log(1-u)\ge-2u\) for \(0\le u\le1/2\),

\[
 \Pr[\operatorname{Sup}_s(T)=\varnothing]
       \ge \exp(-14mC^4p^3),
 \quad C^2p\le\tfrac12,\quad p^3\le\tfrac12.                 \tag{13}
\]

Repeated boundaries cause no problem: (12) still holds, and counting an
event more than once only weakens the product lower bound.

Let \(\mu=pn\) and \(a=14mC^4p^3\). The elementary binomial tail bounds give

\[
       \Pr[|T|\notin[\mu/2,2\mu]]\le2e^{-\mu/8}.              \tag{14}
\]

Whenever \(2e^{-\mu/8}\le\tfrac12e^{-a}\), there is an integer
\(k\in[\mu/2,2\mu]\) satisfying

\[
                |\mathcal G_k|\ge\tfrac12e^{-a}\binom nk.
                                                               \tag{15}
\]

Indeed, condition on \(|T|=k\), where the distribution is uniform over
\(k\)-sets. The total mass of good sets within the interval is at least
\(\tfrac12e^{-a}\). This is a weighted sum of the conditional good-set
fractions, with total weight at most one. At least one such fraction is at
least \(\tfrac12e^{-a}\). No unproved concentration property of the
conditioned good-set distribution is needed.

## 4. Add a fixed occurrence bound to the phase-16 family

The [phase-16 construction](correlation_proof_boundary.md#4-a-scalable-control-in-the-same-proper-linear-3-cnf-class)
selects each triple independently with probability \(96/n^2\). With
probability tending to one it has at least \(15n\) triples, every set of at
most \(s=\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor\), \(\varepsilon=10^{-16}\), has
neighborhood larger than \(7|A|/4\), and there are at most \(\sqrt n\) pairs
of triples sharing two variables. These are the established estimates in
that construction.

There is a simultaneous positive-probability event allowing a fixed maximum
occurrence. Let \(d_v\) be the sampled degree of variable \(v\). Its mean is
less than \(48\), and

\[
       \mathbb E[d_v^2]
       =\mathbb E[d_v(d_v-1)]+\mathbb E[d_v]<48^2+48=2352.
\]

Markov's inequality shows that
\(\sum_v d_v^2\le8192n\) holds with probability at least
\(1-2352/8192>0\). Intersecting with the preceding events still has positive
probability for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Delete every triple touching a vertex whose sampled degree is greater than
\(\Delta=4096\). The number deleted is at most

\[
 \sum_{v:d_v>\Delta}d_v
          \le\frac{\sum_v d_v^2}{\Delta}\le2n.                 \tag{16}
\]

Next remove one triple from each original bad pair, at a cost at most
\(\sqrt n\), and retain exactly \(12n\) triples. This is possible for large
\(n\), since \(15n-2n-\sqrt n\ge12n\). Every surviving variable has degree
at most \(4096\); the supports are linear. Deletion preserves every required
neighborhood-expansion inequality. Incidence counting gives
\(|\partial A|\ge2|N(A)|-3|A|>|A|/2\), so (1) holds.

Independent uniform signs have expected number of satisfying assignments
\(2^n(7/8)^{12n}<1\). Hence an unsatisfiable signing exists. All-positive
signs give a satisfiable sibling on exactly the same bounded-occurrence
supports. The proof of (4) and the counts (11)–(15) hold for either signing.
The family remains existential; this is not a practical finite generator.

**Formalization update (2026-11-08 JST).** The Lean construction keeps the
same sampling probability and family constants, but makes the simultaneous
event finite and explicit for every \(n\ge10^6\). It bounds expansion failure
by \(1/4\), the lower edge-count tail by \(1/16\), the degree-square expectation
by \(2352n\), and the expected number of **ordered** bad pairs by 4608.
One averaging argument supplies a sample with at least \(15n\) triples,
degree-square sum at most \(8192n\), at most \(n\) ordered bad pairs, and all
required neighborhood inequalities. Deleting all high-degree incidences costs
at most \(2n\) triples; deleting all bad-pair endpoints costs at most \(n\),
because each deleted endpoint is the first coordinate of an ordered bad pair.
Exactly \(12n\) triples can then be retained. The
[detailed proof map](phase20_formalization.md) records the counting and signing
theorems; no asymptotic probability assertion is assumed by the Lean endpoint.

## 5. Exponential bound on distinct high-rank false flats

This section applies the [common-annihilator criterion](affine_dag_annihilator.md)
to unrestricted DAGs of affine clauses over the original variables and
\(\mathbb F_2\). Initial clauses are the raw CNF clauses; every other node is
a semantic consequence of at most two earlier nodes. Arbitrary reuse of
earlier nodes is allowed. In particular, the model includes ordinary
unrestricted resolution over parities with semantic weakening.

Let \(S\) be the number of nodes and let \(H_R\) count the **distinct nonempty
falsifying affine flats** among nodes whose flats have codimension greater
than \(R\). Duplicate flats are counted once. The companion criterion gives

\[
 \dim U\le
 \sum_{\substack{Z\text{ distinct false flat}\\
                         \operatorname{codim}Z>R}}
       \mathcal B_k(\dim Z)
 \le H_R\,\mathcal B_k(n-R-1),\qquad
 \mathcal B_k(N)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k,N)}\binom Nj,                \tag{17}
\]

for every degree-at-most-\(k\) space \(U\) separated from ordinary
\(C_{k+3R}(F)\), whenever input width is at most \(R\). Equation (17) is the
common-annihilator criterion: high-rank false affine flats impose the
restriction maps, and low-rank nodes admit an ordinary-PC simulation
weighted by the resulting common polynomial. The full rank profile in (17)
is useful independently of a uniform rank threshold. Since \(H_R\le S\),
any lower bound on \(H_R\) is also a node-count lower bound. This note does
not use the different registry-based preprint criterion as a premise.

Let \(m=12n\), \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), \(s=\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor\),
and retain (1) and occurrence bound \(\Delta\). With \(C\) as in (6), set

\[
       p=\frac{\varepsilon}{1024C^2},\qquad pn\ge16.
                                                               \tag{18}
\]

Then \(C^2p\le1/1024\), and

\[
 a=168nC^4p^3\le\frac{\varepsilon pn}{4096}
                      \le\frac{pn}{4096}.
                                                               \tag{19}
\]

The last condition in (15) follows from \(pn\ge16\), since
\(2e^{-pn/8}\le\tfrac12e^{-pn/4096}\le\tfrac12e^{-a}\).
Choose the integer \(k\in[pn/2,2pn]\) supplied by (15), and put
\(R=\lfloor s/16\rfloor\). These choices imply

\[
 s\ge\varepsilon n/2,\quad R\ge3,\quad
 k\le s/256,\quad
 k+3R\le49s/256<s/4.                                        \tag{20}
\]

Thus (4) supplies the separated \(U_k\) required by (17). Write
\(N=n-R-1\). Here \(k\le(N+1)/3\), and the ratio of consecutive binomial
coefficients gives

\[
 \mathcal B_k(N)
 \le\binom Nk\frac{N-k+1}{N-2k+1}
 \le2\binom Nk,
 \qquad
 \frac{\binom nk}{\binom Nk}
   =\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{n-j}{n-R-1-j}
   \ge\exp\left(\frac{(R+1)k}{n}\right).                     \tag{21}
\]

The denominators are positive under (20). Applying (15), (17), and (21),
then using \(R+1>s/16\ge\varepsilon n/32\) and \(k\ge pn/2\), yields

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_R&\ge\tfrac14\exp\left(\frac{(R+1)k}{n}-a\right)\\
  &\ge\tfrac14\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon pn}{128}\right)
   =\boxed{\tfrac14\exp\left(
                  \frac{\varepsilon^2 n}{131072C^2}\right)}.
 \end{aligned}                                               \tag{22}
\]

Thus both the number of distinct high-rank false flats and the number of
proof nodes are exponential in \(n\). The concrete F2 specialization below,
including support-family existence, is now a Lean theorem for the stated
proof system. The general-field statements elsewhere in this note retain
their paper-proof scope unless separately identified as formalized.

For the bounded-occurrence phase-16 refinement one may take
\(\Delta=4096\), \(q=12288\), \(C=12288^2\), and
\(\varepsilon=10^{-16}\). The exponent coefficient in (22) is exactly
\(1/(81\cdot2^{65}\cdot10^{32})\), approximately \(3.35\times10^{-54}\).
It is a positive asymptotic constant, deliberately far from a useful
benchmark prediction. The graph existence statement also has its own
"sufficiently large \(n\)" threshold.

The crucial gain over a bare degree lower bound is (15): a large, explicitly
described span of monomials survives the low-degree consequence space. The
point \(1\notin C_B(F)\) alone would not establish (17)'s needed dimension.

## 6. What has and has not been established here

- The separated-space conclusion (4) uses a published reduction operator,
  with its ordinary-PC applicability checked explicitly.
- The finite counting estimates, positive-association proof, and the
  bounded-occurrence refinement are mathematical arguments above, not
  conclusions inferred from experiments.
- Equation (22) is a consequence of the exact criterion (17); its use for a
  named proof system uses the companion proof of that criterion with the
  semantic binary affine-clause rules and ordinary-PC degree convention.
- A restricted proof-system lower bound does not exclude a polynomial-time
  SAT algorithm using a different reasoning mechanism. No P versus NP
  solution or universal algorithmic lower bound is asserted.
