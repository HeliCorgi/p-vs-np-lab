# Let the Boolean clause table suggest the characteristic

Given a signed 3-CNF with no encoding metadata, can we detect a prime at which its **local** equations become stronger, without first trying a hand-picked list of fields? Yes on the observed scopes. This is an exact integer-matrix calculation. It is not a SAT test: a balanced flow and an inconsistent flow can exhibit the same local prime.

## A finite list containing every exceptional prime

Let S be any enumerated set of satisfying assignments to the *local* clauses on d≤9 Boolean coordinates. Put one integer row (x,1) into M for each x in S. Over a field K, the vector space of affine equations valid on S is exactly the right kernel of M: dotting x with the coefficient vector and adding its constant offset must give zero. Define p to be **exceptional for S** when rank over F_p is strictly less than rank over the rationals. This compares dimensions; it does not confuse the ordinary one-hot integer laws with new characteristic-specific information.

Let r=rank_Q(M). Any (r+1)-minor vanishes as an integer, and still vanishes modulo every p, so rank_p(M)≤r. Select *one* integer r×r minor with nonzero determinant D. If p does not divide D, that minor is invertible modulo p, giving rank_p(M)=r. Thus **the only candidates are the prime divisors of |D|**. Test the full modular rank at each divisor: other minors may retain rank r. Equivalently p is exceptional iff it divides the gcd of all r-minors, but computing every minor is unnecessary. For empty S the empty minor is 1 by convention, no prime is exceptional, and the inconsistent local scope is explicitly counted. For singleton S there are already d independent local affine equations over every field; there need be no exceptional prime there either.

The integer kernel is saturated in the following sense. Integer solutions to Mz=0 form a free abelian group, and if pz is a solution, so is z. Consequently the reduction of an integral **lattice basis** of that kernel has rational-kernel dimension over every F_p: a dependence with coefficients not all multiples of p would say a basis combination is p times an ambient vector, necessarily p times another kernel vector, contradicting uniqueness of basis coordinates. A rank drop therefore counts genuinely additional independent affine equations, beyond this reduced integer-kernel subspace. Merely clearing denominators in an arbitrary rational basis is not necessarily enough to construct a lattice basis. The implementation needs neither a lattice basis nor any factorization oracle.

[The scope finder](modular_hull.md) uses only raw clauses, and enumerates their exact Boolean satisfying words within each selected scope. Discovery takes independent rows via integer elimination, selects pivot columns and computes exact determinants using [Bareiss's fraction-free algorithm (1968)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1968-0226829-0). Each minor has r≤10 and Boolean entries, so by the row-norm determinant bound, |D|≤r^(r/2)≤100,000. Deterministic trial division through the square root of the remaining integer suffices here. The work per fixed-size local table is bounded, apart from locating its original clauses; there is no corresponding claim about fast general integer factorization on large global matrices. A scan limit reports whether all *proposed* scopes were visited. Even an uncapped scan is not an exhaustive scan of every subset of nine variables unless the configured finder enumerates them all.

## What exactly the local rank witness checks

The witness identifies the satisfying words and columns of one nonsingular rank-sized minor over Q, its signed integer determinant, and analogous minors/ranks for each candidate prime. Its replayer recomputes the table by evaluating the **original clauses entirely supported on the specified named variables**, rather than trusting a submitted table, scope generator, or modular elimination. A nonzero s-minor proves rank≥s. To prove rank≤s, the checker tests that bordering this minor by each unused row and each unused column gives zero determinant in the claimed field. This suffices: use the invertible minor to match any row on the pivot columns; the border determinants certify equality on all remaining columns. The displayed modular-prime list must match every factor of the rational witness determinant, and the exceptional list matches the checked ranks. The checker validates *one local relation*, not a claim that an input has no other exceptional scope. For an independent bounded oracle the tests enumerate all 256 three-bit truth tables and compare against gcds of all maximal minors evaluated using permutation sums.

Two useful pitfalls are tested. The three words 011,101,110 give a chosen rational 3-minor of magnitude two, but a different minor has magnitude one, so **2 is not exceptional**. Conversely a proper seven-variable, 59-clause CNF with eight models gives an 8×8 minor of determinant 10 and rank seven modulo both 2 and 5. The scan returns **[2,5]**, including a prime unsupported by the current F2/F3 SAT backends. The spectrum never asserts UNSAT from that observation alone.

## All-size positive controls and a global false negative

On a ternary cubic star the complete one-hot table has three ordinary one-hot sum equations. Any additive equation beyond these, after recentering the three coefficient functions, corresponds to an additive homomorphism from Z/3Z to the additive group of the coefficient field. It can be nonzero exactly in characteristic 3. The analogous binary parity star picks out 2; see [the signed-clause construction and proof](ternary_flow.md). Coordinate complements and permutations act by unimodular affine transformations of the augmented word matrix and do not change its ranks in any characteristic. Thus the true full-star scope has the matching exceptional prime independently of scrambling or charges. Scanning all proposed smaller or overlapping scopes can in general produce further primes; the reported network experiments check their complete union rather than promising none for arbitrary distractors. On twelve balanced and unbalanced renamed networks the discovered singleton spectra were respectively [2] for binary flows and [3] for ternary flows. With 64 prism positions and ternary encoding the program visited 7,808 scopes, of which 128 had a genuine local rank drop. Calling the independent [UNSAT certificate backend](modular_hull.md) afterward is a *separate* inference. Every balanced and unbalanced twin was treated with exactly the same data-only rank rule.

**Do not omit a global field just because its local rank never dropped.** Form an odd cycle of length >9 with original binary clauses encoding x_i+x_(i+1)=1 as equality of integer values. Every scope of ≤9 variables induces paths and isolated vertices. Each component can choose its starting bit freely and the rest is determined by alternating x and 1−x. Taking one base assignment and one full component flip per component gives a unit-magnitude minor on representatives plus the constant column; the integral parameterization bounds rank in the other direction. Thus no such scope has *any* exceptional prime. Yet adding the edge equations modulo two gives 0=1, while the rational linear equations have solution x_i=1/2 (and in any odd prime field x_i=2^{-1}). The tested eleven-cycle returns an empty spectrum and a checked mod-two UNSAT refutation. A local rank-drop is sufficient evidence to **try** that field, not a necessary condition for such a field to matter after composition. An integer treatment of the globally assembled equations or an explicit fallback is a separate algorithmic task.
